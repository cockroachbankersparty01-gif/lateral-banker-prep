## Rates in force on 25 November 2026

| Instrument | Rate | What it means |
|---|---|---|

## The monetary policy framework

- **Legal basis:** Chapter IIIF of the RBI Act, 1934 (Sections 45Z to 45ZO), inserted by the Finance Act, 2016. The primary objective is price stability while keeping growth in mind.
- **Inflation target (Sec 45ZA):** set by the Central Government in consultation with RBI, once every five years. For **1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031** the target is again **4% CPI (combined) inflation, with a band of 2% to 6%** (notified 25 March 2026). This is the second time the original 2016 target has been retained.
- **Failure (Sec 45ZN):** if average inflation stays above 6% or below 2% for three consecutive quarters, RBI must report to the Government the reasons, the remedial action and the time it expects to take. The first such report was sent in November 2022.

### Monetary Policy Committee (Sec 45ZB)

- Six members: the Governor (chairperson), the Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, one RBI officer nominated by the Central Board, and three external experts appointed by the Central Government.
- External members serve four years and cannot be re-appointed.
- Quorum is four members, including the Governor (or the Deputy Governor in his absence). Each member has one vote; the Governor has a second (casting) vote in a tie.
- At least four meetings a year (in practice six). The resolution is published after each meeting and the **minutes on the 14th day** (Sec 45ZL).
- Members in 2026: Sanjay Malhotra (Governor), Dr Poonam Gupta (Deputy Governor), Indranil Bhattacharyya (Executive Director); external members Dr Nagesh Kumar, Saugata Bhattacharya and Prof. Ram Singh.

## The policy toolkit

### Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)

- Introduced in June 2000 on the recommendation of the Narasimham Committee II. It covers repo, reverse repo, SDF and MSF operations.
- **Corridor:** SDF (floor) = repo − 25 bps; MSF (ceiling) = repo + 25 bps. The corridor is 50 bps wide and the weighted average call rate is the operating target.
- **Variable rate repo / reverse repo (VRR / VRRR) auctions** are used to fine-tune liquidity on top of the fixed-rate window.
- **SDF** was introduced on 8 April 2022 under Sec 17(2A) of the RBI Act. It absorbs liquidity without RBI having to give securities, and it replaced the fixed rate reverse repo as the floor.
- **Fixed Rate Reverse Repo (FRRR)** stays in the toolkit at RBI's discretion; it was 3.35% when it was moved out of the corridor.

### Marginal Standing Facility

- Started in May 2011. Scheduled commercial banks can borrow overnight at MSF rate, dipping into their SLR holdings up to 2% of NDTL.
- It is a penal, last-resort window: a bank uses it when the inter-bank market cannot meet its needs.

### Reserve requirements

| | CRR | SLR |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Sec 42(1), RBI Act | Sec 24, BR Act |
| Limits | No floor or ceiling since the 2006 amendment | Maximum 40% of NDTL; no floor since 2007 |
| Kept in | Cash balance with RBI | Cash, gold (valued at not more than current market price), unencumbered approved securities |
| Interest | None | Yield on the securities held |
| Maintenance | Fortnightly average on the NDTL of the second preceding fortnight; daily balance at least 90% of the requirement | Daily, on the NDTL of the second preceding fortnight |
| Default | Penal interest at Bank Rate + 3%, rising to Bank Rate + 5% if it continues | Penal interest at Bank Rate + 3% |

- **NDTL** = demand and time liabilities to others (deposits, other borrowings, other liabilities) **plus** net liabilities to the banking system (liabilities to banks minus assets with banks).
- **Exempt from CRR and SLR:** special incremental FCNR(B)/NRE deposits notified by RBI (see the 2026 update below) and eligible long-term bonds raised for infrastructure and affordable housing.

> **2026 update:** To attract foreign currency inflows, RBI exempted from CRR and SLR fresh **FCNR(B) deposits of 3–5 years** mobilised from 8 June 2026 and fresh **NRE term deposits of 3 years or more** mobilised from 19 June 2026. The window, first set to close on 30 September 2026, was cut short to **31 August 2026**. A transfer from an NRO account to an NRE account does not qualify. RBI also offered banks a US dollar–rupee swap facility for such fresh FCNR(B) funds and for eligible ECBs.

### Other instruments

- **Open Market Operations (OMO):** outright purchase (injects liquidity) or sale (absorbs liquidity) of G-secs.
- **Forex swaps:** buy/sell dollar–rupee swaps inject rupee liquidity for a fixed period; sell/buy swaps absorb it.
- **Bank Rate (Sec 49, RBI Act):** the standard rate at which RBI discounts eligible bills; now aligned with MSF and used for penalties.
- **Market Stabilisation Scheme (MSS):** special T-bills and dated securities issued to absorb surplus liquidity from forex inflows; the proceeds stay with RBI.
- **Moral suasion** and **selective credit control** (Sec 21 of the BR Act) are qualitative tools.

## Lending-rate systems in India

| System | From | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| BPLR | 2003 | Benchmark Prime Lending Rate set by each bank |
| Base Rate | 1 Jul 2010 | Minimum rate; lending below it not allowed |
| MCLR | 1 Apr 2016 | Marginal cost of funds based; reset period up to 1 year |
| External benchmark (EBLR) | 1 Oct 2019 | New floating loans to individuals and MSEs linked to repo, 3- or 6-month T-bill yield or another FBIL benchmark; reset at least once every three months |

> **Exam tip:** Under EBLR the spread over the benchmark can change only if the borrower's credit risk changes substantially. Operating costs may be changed once in three years.

## Quick recap

- Repo 5.50%, SDF 5.25%, MSF/Bank Rate 5.75%, CRR 3%, SLR 18% (from 7 October 2026).
- Inflation target 4% ± 2% for April 2026 – March 2031; failure = outside the band for three straight quarters.
- MPC: 6 members, quorum 4, Governor's casting vote, minutes on day 14.
- CRR: Sec 42 RBI Act, no interest; SLR: Sec 24 BR Act, maximum 40%.
- EBLR loans reset at least once in three months.
