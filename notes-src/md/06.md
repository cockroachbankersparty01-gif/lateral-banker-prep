# Priority Sector Lending

## Background

- The idea of directing credit to neglected sectors came with social control of banks (1968) and the **Informal Study Group** (1971–72), which first defined the "priority sector".
- The **Dr K.S. Krishnaswamy Working Group** (1980) set the **40%** target, to be reached by 1985.
- Major revisions: M.V. Nair Committee (2012), 2015, September 2020 (district weights, start-ups, renewable energy) and the **Master Directions on PSL, 2025** (24 March 2025, effective **1 April 2025**), issued under Sec 21 and 35A of the BR Act.

## Targets (percentage of ANBC or CEOBE, whichever is higher)

| Category | Domestic banks & foreign banks with 20+ branches | Foreign banks with < 20 branches | RRBs | SFBs | UCBs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total priority sector | **40%** | 40% (up to 32% via exports) | **75%** | **60%** | 60% |
| Agriculture | 18% | – | 18% | 18% | – |
| Small & marginal farmers | 10% | – | 10% | 10% | – |
| Micro enterprises | 7.5% | – | 7.5% | 7.5% | 7.5% |
| Weaker sections | **12%** | – | 15% | 12% | 12% |

- Targets are computed on ANBC or CEOBE **as on the corresponding date of the previous year**. Achievement is the **average of the four quarter-end positions** of the year.
- For domestic banks, a sub-target for **non-corporate individual farmers** is also set, based on the system-wide average of the last three years and notified by RBI.
- SFBs moved from 75% to 60% from FY 2025-26. The weaker-sections target for commercial banks rose from 10% to 12% in the 2025 directions.

> **Exam tip:** "ANBC or CEOBE, whichever is higher" – CEOBE (credit equivalent of off-balance-sheet exposures) matters mainly for foreign banks with large derivative books.

## Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC)

- **Bank credit in India** (as defined in Sec 42(2) of the RBI Act)
- **minus** bills rediscounted with RBI and other approved financial institutions = **Net Bank Credit (NBC)**
- **plus** outstanding deposits in RIDF and other funds (placed for PSL shortfall), outstanding PSLCs bought, and eligible non-SLR investments held to maturity
- **minus** permitted exemptions (e.g., eligible long-term infrastructure and affordable housing bonds; advances against the special FCNR(B)/NRE deposits exempt from CRR/SLR in 2026).

## District weights

- To spread credit more evenly, RBI ranks districts by per capita priority sector credit. From FY 2024-25, incremental PSL credit in districts with per capita PSL **below ₹9,000** gets a weight of **125%**; in districts **above ₹42,000** it gets **90%**; others 100%.
- RRBs, UCBs, LABs and foreign banks (including subsidiaries) are exempt from the weights.

## The eight categories

### 1. Agriculture

| Sub-category | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Farm credit** | Crop loans and KCC; medium and long-term loans for agriculture and allied activities (dairy, fisheries, poultry, beekeeping); loans to distressed farmers indebted to moneylenders; loans against pledge of produce for up to 12 months – up to **₹90 lakh** against negotiable warehouse receipts (NWR/e-NWR) and **₹60 lakh** against other receipts; solar pumps and solar plants on farmers' fallow land. Loans to corporate farmers, FPOs/FPCs, partnership firms and co-operatives of farmers count up to an aggregate limit per borrowing entity |
| **Agriculture infrastructure** | Storage, cold chains, soil conservation, plant tissue culture, seed production – up to **₹100 crore** per borrower |
| **Ancillary activities** | Agri-clinics and agri-business centres, food and agro-processing, custom service units, loans to PACS/FSS/LAMPS for on-lending, **agri start-ups up to ₹50 crore** |

- **Small farmer:** land of 1–2 hectares; **marginal farmer:** up to 1 hectare. Landless labourers, tenant farmers, oral lessees and sharecroppers with small holdings are treated as small and marginal farmers.

### 2. Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises

- **All** bank loans to MSMEs (manufacturing and services, as per the Udyam certificate) qualify. Retail and wholesale trade are treated as MSME for PSL since July 2021.
- Also: loans to Khadi and Village Industries; factoring with recourse where the assignor is an MSME; loans to start-ups up to ₹50 crore; bank loans to NBFCs for on-lending to MSEs (within the on-lending cap).

### 3. Export credit

- Domestic banks: incremental export credit over the base date, up to **2% of ANBC**. Foreign banks with fewer than 20 branches: up to **32% of ANBC**.

### 4. Education

- Loans to individuals for education including vocational courses, up to **₹25 lakh** per borrower (raised from ₹20 lakh in 2025).

### 5. Housing

| Centre population | Loan limit | Dwelling cost |
|---|---|---|
| 50 lakh and above | ₹50 lakh | Up to ₹63 lakh |
| 10 lakh to below 50 lakh | ₹45 lakh | Up to ₹57 lakh |
| Below 10 lakh | ₹35 lakh | Up to ₹44 lakh |

- Population is taken from **Census 2011** (urban agglomeration or town); villages not in the census table use the "below 10 lakh" limits.
- Also: loans for repair of houses within limits, loans to government agencies for slum clearance and housing for economically weaker sections, and bank loans to HFCs for on-lending within the cap.

### 6. Social infrastructure

- Loans up to **₹8 crore** per borrower for schools, health care facilities, drinking water, sanitation, including in Tier II–VI centres; loans to MFIs for on-lending for these purposes.

### 7. Renewable energy

- Up to **₹35 crore** per borrower for solar, biomass, wind, micro-hydel and non-conventional public utilities; up to **₹10 lakh** per household.

### 8. Others

- Loans to SHGs and JLGs (other than for agriculture or MSME purposes), loans to distressed persons to repay non-institutional lenders, loans to State-sponsored organisations for SC/ST for on-lending.

## Weaker sections

Loans to the following count towards the 12% weaker-sections target:

- Small and marginal farmers; artisans and village and cottage industries with small individual credit limits.
- Beneficiaries of government schemes (DAY-NRLM, DAY-NULM, self-employment schemes for manual scavengers), DRI beneficiaries, SHGs.
- SC/ST persons, minority communities, persons with disabilities, individual women beneficiaries (within the prescribed limit), **transgender persons** (added in 2025) and distressed farmers.

## On-lending, co-lending and buying PSL loans

- **On-lending through NBFCs and HFCs:** bank loans to NBFCs for on-lending to agriculture, MSEs and housing count as PSL within per-borrower limits, but such loans together are capped at **5%** of the bank's total priority sector lending.
- **Co-lending:** under the Co-lending Directions, 2025 (from 1 January 2026), a bank and an NBFC jointly lend; the bank's share of a priority sector loan counts as PSL.
- **Securitisation and direct assignment:** PSL portfolios bought from NBFCs count if they meet the conditions (genuine PSL assets, a cap on the interest charged to the end borrower, no re-sale).
- **Bank loans to MFIs** for on-lending to individuals qualify within RBI's conditions.

## Priority Sector Lending Certificates (PSLCs)

- Introduced in April 2016. A bank that has over-achieved sells **only the achievement**, not the loan or its risk; the buyer counts it towards its target.
- **Four kinds:** PSLC-Agriculture, PSLC-Small and Marginal Farmers, PSLC-Micro Enterprises and PSLC-General.
- Traded on RBI's **e-Kuber** platform in lots of ₹25 lakh at a premium set by the market; all certificates **expire on 31 March**.

| PSLC | Counts towards |
|---|---|
| Agriculture | Agriculture target |
| Small and marginal farmers | SMF sub-target and agriculture target |
| Micro enterprises | Micro enterprises sub-target |
| General | Overall priority sector target |

## Shortfall in targets

- The shortfall must be deposited in the **Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)** with NABARD and other funds with NABARD, NHB, SIDBI or MUDRA, as allocated by RBI. These deposits earn interest well below market rates, and the shortfall is also considered when RBI grants regulatory approvals.

## Quick recap

- 40% overall; agriculture 18%; SMF 10%; micro 7.5%; weaker sections 12% (RRBs 75% and 15%; SFBs and UCBs 60%).
- Base: ANBC or CEOBE of the previous year; achievement = average of four quarters.
- Limits: education ₹25 lakh; housing ₹50/45/35 lakh; social infrastructure ₹8 crore; renewable energy ₹35 crore (₹10 lakh per household); agri infrastructure ₹100 crore; start-ups ₹50 crore.
- District weights: 125% below ₹9,000 per capita; 90% above ₹42,000.
- Four PSLCs, e-Kuber, expire 31 March; shortfall to RIDF.

# MSME Finance and Credit Guarantees

## Who is an MSME (from 1 April 2025)

| Enterprise | Investment in plant & machinery / equipment | Turnover |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Up to ₹2.5 crore | Up to ₹10 crore |
| Small | Up to ₹25 crore | Up to ₹100 crore |
| Medium | Up to ₹125 crore | Up to ₹500 crore |

- Both conditions must be met (composite criteria since 1 July 2020). Export turnover is excluded from turnover.
- Registration is online on the **Udyam** portal (free, based on Aadhaar, PAN and GST data). Informal micro units without GST can use the **Udyam Assist Platform** (2023); its certificate is accepted for PSL.
- **MSMED Act, 2006:** a buyer must pay an MSE supplier within the agreed period, **not more than 45 days**; for delay, compound interest at **three times the bank rate** with monthly rests (Sec 15–16). Since 2024, the Income-tax law allows the buyer a deduction for such purchases only if paid within the time limit.

## RBI rules on lending to MSEs

- **No collateral** for loans to micro and small enterprises up to **₹20 lakh** (for loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 April 2026; earlier ₹10 lakh). A voluntary pledge of gold or silver by the borrower is not treated as breaking this rule.
- **Working capital – turnover method** (Nayak Committee, 1992): for fund-based limits up to **₹5 crore**, working capital need = **25%** of projected annual turnover; the bank finances at least **20%** and the borrower brings **5%** as margin.
- A **composite loan** (term loan plus working capital) up to ₹1 crore can be given through a single window.
- **Revival and rehabilitation (2016 framework):** banks identify incipient stress through SMA classes and refer stressed MSMEs with exposure up to ₹25 crore to a **Committee for Stressed MSMEs** for a corrective action plan.
- Banks must acknowledge MSE loan applications and decide within set time frames; reasons for rejection are recorded.

## TReDS – Trade Receivables Discounting System

- An online platform where MSME sellers' invoices accepted by large buyers are auctioned to financiers – **without recourse** to the MSME. First platforms (2017): RXIL, A.TReDS (Invoicemart) and M1xchange.
- Companies with turnover above **₹250 crore** and all CPSEs must register on TReDS.
- **TReDS Directions, 2026:** minimum net worth of the operator **₹25 crore** (existing operators by 31 March 2028); invoices and bills once accepted by the buyer (factoring units) carry the buyer's **unconditional obligation** to pay on the due date; the buyer cannot set off quality disputes; assignment of receivables is registered with **CERSAI**; multiple financiers bid; re-discounting and credit insurance are allowed.

## Credit guarantee schemes

### CGTMSE

- **Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises**, set up in **2000** by the Government of India and **SIDBI**.
- Guarantees **collateral-free** credit (term loans and working capital) by member lending institutions to new and existing MSEs, including retail trade.
- **Maximum cover: ₹10 crore** per borrower (from 1 April 2025; earlier ₹5 crore).

| Borrower / loan | Extent of guarantee |
|---|---|
| Micro enterprises, loans up to ₹5 lakh | 85% |
| Women, SC/ST entrepreneurs, units in the North-East, persons with disabilities and other notified categories | 85% |
| Others (above ₹5 lakh, up to ₹10 crore) | 75% |

- **Annual guarantee fee** is paid by the lender (and may be passed on); it starts at **0.37% a year** for small loans and rises with loan size, with concessions for special categories.
- **Lock-in:** a claim can be made only after **18 months** from the last disbursement (**9 months** for guarantees up to ₹10 lakh with tenor up to 36 months).
- **Claim settlement:** **75%** of the guaranteed amount is paid on the first claim; the balance **25%** after recovery proceedings end. Legal action is waived for claims up to **₹10 lakh** (from 1 April 2023).

### Other guarantee funds

| Scheme | Key points |
|---|---|
| **CGFMU** (NCGTC) | Guarantees Mudra loans (Shishu, Kishore, Tarun, Tarun Plus) and overdrafts under PMJDY |
| **CGSS** – Credit Guarantee Scheme for Start-ups (NCGTC, 6 October 2022) | Cover up to **₹20 crore** per DPIIT-recognised start-up (earlier ₹10 crore); fee cut to **1% a year** for start-ups in 27 "champion sectors" |
| **CGFSEL** | Skill development loans (NCGTC) |
| **CGFEL** | Education loans (NCGTC) |
| **ECLGS** | Emergency credit line during COVID-19; **ECLGS 5.0** in 2026 (0% risk weight on 75% of the guaranteed portion) |

> **Remember:** NCGTC (National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company) was set up by the Government in 2014 to run several guarantee funds; CGTMSE is a separate trust promoted with SIDBI.

## Government schemes for enterprises

### PMEGP – Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme

- Launched in **August 2008** by merging PMRY and REGP; run by **KVIC** (nodal), with State KVIBs and District Industries Centres. Continued for **2021-22 to 2025-26** under the 15th Finance Commission cycle.
- **Eligibility:** individuals above 18 years; education up to class VIII for projects above ₹10 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹5 lakh (service); only **new** projects; SHGs, trusts, co-operatives and societies also eligible.
- **Maximum project cost:** **₹50 lakh** for manufacturing and **₹20 lakh** for service/business units.

| Category | Own contribution | Subsidy – urban | Subsidy – rural |
|---|---|---|---|
| General | 10% | 15% | 25% |
| Special (SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women, ex-servicemen, PwD, NER, hill and border areas, transgenders) | 5% | 25% | 35% |

- Bank finances the balance (90% or 95%). No collateral is required within the collateral-free limit; PMEGP loans are covered by CGTMSE.
- The subsidy (margin money) is kept as a **term deposit for 3 years** (lock-in) and adjusted to the loan after the unit is physically verified (with geo-tagging) and is working.
- **EDP training:** at least 5 days for projects up to ₹5 lakh and 10 days for bigger projects (optional up to ₹2 lakh).
- **Second loan for upgradation:** up to **₹1 crore** (manufacturing) or **₹25 lakh** (service) for successful units, with **15% subsidy (20% in NER and hill States)** and 10% own contribution.
- Repayment over 3 to 7 years after a moratorium.

### Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana (PMMY)

- Launched **8 April 2015** for non-farm (and allied agri) income-generating micro enterprises; loans by banks, NBFCs and MFIs, refinanced by MUDRA (a SIDBI subsidiary).

| Category | Loan |
|---|---|
| Shishu | Up to ₹50,000 |
| Kishore | Above ₹50,000 up to ₹5 lakh |
| Tarun | Above ₹5 lakh up to ₹10 lakh |
| Tarun Plus (from October 2024) | Above ₹10 lakh up to ₹20 lakh – for those who have repaid a Tarun loan |

- No collateral; covered by CGFMU; working capital can be given through a **MUDRA RuPay card**.

### Stand-Up India (5 April 2016)

- Composite loans of **₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore** for a **greenfield** (first-time) enterprise in manufacturing, services, trading or allied agriculture.
- Each bank branch must give at least **one loan to an SC/ST borrower and one to a woman borrower**. For non-individual units, at least 51% must be held by such persons.
- Bank finance up to 85% of the project cost, with the borrower bringing at least 10% as own contribution; repayment within 7 years with a moratorium of up to 18 months; covered by a credit guarantee fund run by NCGTC.

### PM Vishwakarma (17 September 2023)

- For artisans and craftspeople in **18 traditional trades** (carpenter, blacksmith, potter, cobbler, tailor, etc.).
- Collateral-free loans of **₹1 lakh** (first tranche, 18 months) and **₹2 lakh** (second tranche, 30 months) at a concessional **5%** interest, with Government subvention and credit guarantee; toolkit incentive of ₹15,000; skill training with a stipend.

## Quick recap

- Micro ₹2.5 crore / ₹10 crore; small ₹25 crore / ₹100 crore; medium ₹125 crore / ₹500 crore.
- Collateral-free MSE loans ₹20 lakh (from 1 April 2026); turnover method 25% need, 20% bank, 5% margin.
- MSE payment within 45 days; delay interest 3× bank rate.
- CGTMSE: up to ₹10 crore; 85%/75% cover; lock-in 18 months; 75% + 25% claim.
- PMEGP: ₹50 lakh / ₹20 lakh; subsidy 15–35%; 3-year lock-in. Mudra: ₹50,000 / ₹5 lakh / ₹10 lakh / ₹20 lakh.
