# Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934

## Background

- RBI was set up under the RBI Act, 1934, on the recommendation of the **Hilton Young Commission** (1926). It started operations on **1 April 1935**, with its central office first in Calcutta (moved to Bombay in 1937).
- It began as a shareholders' bank and was **nationalised on 1 January 1949**. Its capital is ₹5 crore, held wholly by the Government.
- The Preamble was amended in 2016 to add the monetary policy framework (flexible inflation targeting).

## Scheduled and non-scheduled banks

- A **scheduled bank** is one included in the **Second Schedule** of the RBI Act. To be included (Sec 42(6)), a bank must have paid-up capital and reserves of at least **₹5 lakh** and must satisfy RBI that its affairs are not run against depositors' interests.
- Scheduled banks can borrow from RBI at the repo window, are members of clearing houses and must keep CRR with RBI. Non-scheduled banks keep cash reserve under Sec 18 of the BR Act.

## RBI's functions at a glance

| Role | Key points |
|---|---|
| Monetary authority | Sets the repo rate through the MPC; manages liquidity |
| Regulator and supervisor | Banks, NBFCs, ARCs, AIFIs, CICs, payment system operators; Board for Financial Supervision (1994) |
| Issuer of currency | Prints and distributes notes; currency chests are run by banks as RBI's agents |
| Banker to Government | Manages accounts and public debt of the Centre and the States |
| Banker's bank | Keeps banks' current accounts; lender of last resort |
| Manager of foreign exchange | Administers FEMA, 1999; manages forex reserves |
| Payment systems | Regulates under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007; runs RTGS and NEFT |
| Development role | Financial inclusion, priority sector, rural credit |

> **Exam tip:** RBI's accounting year was changed to April–March from 2020-21 (it was July–June). The surplus transferred to the Government is decided under the Economic Capital Framework recommended by the Bimal Jalan Committee (2019).

## Quick recap

- RBI started on 1 April 1935; nationalised 1 January 1949; capital ₹5 crore.
- Sec 22 – note issue; Sec 24 – up to ₹10,000; Sec 26(2) – demonetisation.
- Sec 33 – minimum reserves ₹200 crore (gold ≥ ₹115 crore).
- Sec 42 – CRR; Second Schedule bank needs capital and reserves of ₹5 lakh or more.
- Sec 45-IA – NBFC registration; Sec 45ZB – MPC.

# Banking Regulation Act, 1949

## Background

- Passed as the Banking Companies Act, 1949 (in force **16 March 1949**); renamed the Banking Regulation Act from 1 March 1966, when it was extended to co-operative banks.
- The **Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020** brought co-operative banks fully under RBI for management, capital, audit and resolution.
- The **Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025** made the latest changes (substantial interest, nominations, co-operative bank directors' tenure, auditors' pay).

## Key definitions (Section 5)

- **Banking (5(b))** – accepting deposits of money from the public for lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order or otherwise.
- **Banking company (5(c))** – any company that carries on banking business in India.
- **Substantial interest (5(ne))** – holding shares worth more than **₹2 crore** (₹5 lakh before 1 August 2025) or more than 10% of the paid-up capital, whichever is lower.

## Unclaimed deposits and the DEA Fund

- A deposit not operated for **10 years** (or unclaimed for 10 years after maturity) is transferred to the DEA Fund kept by RBI, along with interest. The customer can still claim it any time from the bank, which pays and then claims reimbursement from the Fund.
- Interest payable on unclaimed interest-bearing deposits transferred to the Fund is **3% simple** a year (from 1 July 2018).
- An account with no customer-induced transaction for over two years is **inoperative**; RBI's **UDGAM** portal helps people search unclaimed deposits across banks.
- When a term deposit matures and is not claimed, interest after maturity is paid at the savings rate or the contracted rate, whichever is lower.

## Quick recap

- Substantial interest: over ₹2 crore or 10% of paid-up capital, whichever is lower.
- Sec 9 – non-banking assets sold within 7 years; Sec 17 – 20% to reserve; Sec 24 – SLR up to 40%.
- Sec 12B – 5% acquisition needs RBI approval; Sec 13 – 2.5% commission cap.
- Sec 35A – directions; Sec 36ACA – supersession of Board; Sec 45 – moratorium up to 6 months.
- 45ZA deposits, 45ZC safe custody, 45ZE lockers; up to 4 nominees since 1 November 2025.

# Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

## The three instruments

| | Promissory note (Sec 4) | Bill of exchange (Sec 5) | Cheque (Sec 6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | Unconditional **promise** to pay | Unconditional **order** to pay | Bill drawn on a specified banker, payable on demand |
| Parties | Maker and payee (2) | Drawer, drawee and payee (3) | Drawer, drawee bank and payee (3) |
| Acceptance | Not needed | Needed for usance bills | Not needed |
| Bearer form | Cannot be payable to bearer (Sec 31, RBI Act) | Usance bills cannot be payable to bearer on demand | Can be bearer or order |
| Stamp | Required | Required (usance) | Not required |
| Days of grace | 3 days (usance) | 3 days (usance) | None |

- A cheque includes the **electronic image of a truncated cheque** and a **cheque in electronic form** (added in 2002).
- Essentials of an instrument: in writing, unconditional, signed by the maker/drawer, for a **certain sum of money only**, payable to a certain person or to his order or to bearer. A banknote or currency note is not a promissory note.
- **Sec 13** – negotiable instruments are promissory notes, bills of exchange and cheques payable to order or bearer. By **custom and usage**, drafts, pay orders, dividend and interest warrants, treasury bills and railway receipts are also treated as negotiable.
- **Sec 18** – if the amount in words and figures differs, the amount in **words** is payable.

## Holder and holder in due course

| Point | Holder (Sec 8) | Holder in due course (Sec 9) |
|---|---|---|
| Consideration | Not necessary | Must have paid value |
| Time | Any time | Before the amount became payable (before maturity) |
| Good faith | Not required | Without sufficient cause to believe the transferor's title was defective |
| Title | Gets the transferor's title, with its defects | Gets a good title free from defects of earlier parties |

- A holder in due course can sue all prior parties until the instrument is satisfied (Sec 36). Sec 53 – anyone who takes the instrument from a holder in due course also gets his rights, unless he was party to a fraud.
- **Sec 45A** – a holder can ask the drawer for a duplicate of a lost bill (on giving security).
- **Payment in due course (Sec 10):** payment according to the apparent tenor of the instrument, in good faith and without negligence, to the person in possession, when there is no reason to believe he is not entitled to it.

## Negotiation and endorsement

- **Bearer instruments** are negotiated by **delivery** (Sec 47); **order instruments** by **endorsement and delivery** (Sec 48).
- **Endorsement (Sec 15)** – signing on the back or on an attached slip (allonge) to transfer the instrument.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blank / general | Only the endorser's signature; the instrument becomes payable to bearer |
| Special / full | Names the person to whom it is to be paid ("Pay A or order") |
| Restrictive | Stops further negotiation ("Pay A only") |
| Partial | Transfers only part of the amount – **not valid** (Sec 56) |
| Conditional | Payment depends on an event ("Pay A on his reaching Delhi") |
| Sans recourse (Sec 52) | Endorser excludes his own liability |
| Facultative | Endorser gives up a right, e.g., notice of dishonour |

- A **minor** can draw, endorse and negotiate an instrument, binding everyone except himself (Sec 26).
- A **forged endorsement** passes no title; later parties get no right. But the paying banker is protected under Sec 85(1) if the endorsement looks regular.
- **"Once a bearer, always a bearer":** a cheque originally drawn to bearer stays payable to bearer even if specially endorsed (Sec 85(2)).

## Crossing (Sections 123–131A)

| Type | How | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| General (Sec 123) | Two parallel transverse lines, with or without "& Co." | Paid only through a bank account |
| Special (Sec 124) | Name of a bank written across | Paid only to that bank |
| Not negotiable (Sec 130) | Words "not negotiable" added | Can be transferred, but the transferee gets no better title than the transferor |
| Account payee | "A/c payee" added | Not in the Act; by banking practice and RBI instructions the money goes only to the payee's account |

- **Who can cross (Sec 125):** the drawer; a holder (can cross generally or specially or add "not negotiable"); a bank to which a cheque is crossed specially can cross it again to another bank as its collecting agent.
- A cheque crossed specially to more than one bank is refused, unless the second bank is only an agent for collection (Sec 127).
- A crossing can be cancelled ("opened") only by the **drawer**, under his full signature.
- Demand drafts of **₹20,000 and above** are issued only with "account payee" crossing, and since 15 September 2018 drafts, pay orders and banker's cheques carry the **purchaser's name** on the face.

## Protection to bankers

- **Paying banker:** Sec 10 (payment in due course), **Sec 85(1)** (order cheque – regular endorsement), **Sec 85(2)** (bearer cheque), **Sec 85A** (drafts), **Sec 128** (crossed cheque paid in due course), Sec 89 (alteration not apparent).
- **Collecting banker – Sec 131:** protected against a claim by the true owner if it collected for a **customer**, in **good faith** and **without negligence**. Negligence includes opening an account without proper KYC, or collecting a cheque payable to a company in an individual's account.
- **Sec 31 – drawee bank's duty:** the bank must pay a customer's cheque when it has sufficient funds of the drawer and the cheque is duly presented; if it wrongly refuses, it must compensate the drawer.

## When a bank must not pay a cheque

- Stop-payment instruction from the drawer (countermand).
- Notice of the drawer's death, insanity or insolvency.
- Garnishee or attachment order, or an order from a court, tax authority or investigating agency.
- Cheque is post-dated, stale (more than **3 months** old), mutilated or materially altered.
- Signature differs, funds are insufficient, or the account is closed or frozen.
- Knowledge that the holder's title is defective or that the drawer is committing a breach of trust.

> **Remember:** Under the CTS-2010 standard, no change or correction is allowed on a cheque except **date validation** authenticated by the drawer. For any other change, the customer must issue a fresh cheque.

## Due date of a usance bill

- Add **3 days of grace** (Sec 22) to the date of maturity.
- For bills payable so many **months** after date or sight, the maturity falls on the same date of the month; if that date does not exist, the **last day** of the month is taken (e.g., one month from 31 January = 28/29 February, plus grace = 3 March).
- For bills payable so many **days** after date or sight, the first day is excluded and the last included (Sec 24).
- If the due date falls on a **public holiday**, the bill is payable on the **preceding** business day (Sec 25). Holidays are declared by the Central Government.

## Dishonour of cheques – Section 138

| Step | Time limit |
|---|---|
| Present the cheque | Within its validity of **3 months** |
| Payee sends written demand notice | Within **30 days** of receiving the bank's return memo |
| Drawer must pay | Within **15 days** of receiving the notice |
| File complaint (Sec 142) | Within **1 month** after the 15 days expire (delay can be condoned) |

- Applies only when the cheque was issued for a legally enforceable debt and is returned for insufficient funds or for exceeding the arrangement.
- **Punishment:** imprisonment up to **2 years**, or fine up to **twice** the cheque amount, or both. The offence is compoundable (Sec 147).
- **Jurisdiction (Sec 142(2), 2015):** the court where the payee's bank branch (where the cheque was presented) is located.
- **Sec 143** – summary trial; **Sec 143A** – interim compensation up to **20%** of the cheque amount, payable within 60 days (extendable by 30); **Sec 148** – on appeal, the appellate court can order deposit of at least **20%** of the fine or compensation.
- **Sec 139** – the court presumes the cheque was issued for a debt; **Sec 146** – the bank's return memo is prima facie evidence of dishonour.
- **Noting and protest (Sec 99–100):** a notary records the dishonour of a bill; needed mainly for foreign bills.

## Quick recap

- PN – promise, 2 parties, never bearer; bill – order, 3 parties; cheque – on a banker, on demand.
- Holder in due course: value, before maturity, good faith – gets good title.
- Partial endorsement invalid; "not negotiable" ≠ non-transferable.
- Sec 85 – paying banker; Sec 131 – collecting banker; Sec 10 – payment in due course.
- Sec 138: 30 days notice, 15 days to pay, 1 month to complain; 2 years or twice the amount; 143A and 148 – 20%.
