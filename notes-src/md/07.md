# Agriculture and Rural Credit

## Structure of rural credit

- **Short-term credit** (crop loans) through commercial banks, RRBs and the three-tier co-operative structure: State Co-operative Bank → District Central Co-operative Bank → Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS).
- **Long-term credit** through commercial banks and the land development banks (SCARDBs/PCARDBs).
- **NABARD** (12 July 1982, on the Shivaraman Committee's recommendation) refinances banks for agriculture and rural development, supervises co-operative banks and RRBs, and manages the **Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF, 1995-96)**, which is funded by banks' PSL shortfalls.

## Kisan Credit Card (KCC)

- Introduced in **August 1998** on the recommendation of the **R.V. Gupta Committee**; revised in 2004 and 2012 (T.M. Bhasin Working Group). Extended to animal husbandry and fisheries farmers in 2018-19.
- Covers short-term crop needs, post-harvest expenses, produce marketing loans, household consumption, working capital for farm assets and allied activities, and investment credit.

### How the limit is worked out (current scheme)

1. Scale of finance for the crop × area cultivated.
2. Add **10%** for post-harvest, household and consumption needs.
3. Add **20%** for repairs and maintenance of farm assets.
4. Add crop insurance and asset insurance premium.
5. Increase by **10% a year** for cost escalation over the 5-year validity; the 5th-year limit plus the long-term component is the **maximum permissible limit**.

- Marginal farmers can get a flexible limit of ₹10,000–₹50,000 without the detailed calculation.
- **Collateral:** none up to **₹2 lakh** (raised from ₹1.6 lakh from 1 January 2025); up to ₹3 lakh where there is a tie-up for recovery of dues.
- A **RuPay debit card** is issued; drawals can be made through ATMs, POS and business correspondents.
- **Asset classification:** a crop loan becomes NPA if dues remain unpaid for **two crop seasons** (short-duration crops) or **one crop season** (long-duration crops).

> **2026 update:** RBI's revised **KCC Directions** apply to KCC loans sanctioned from **1 January 2027**: a single **6-year** composite facility covering crop loans, allied activities, working capital and investment needs; crop seasons standardised at **12 months** (short-duration) and **18 months** (long-duration) for repayment and NPA purposes; wider flexi-KCC use for allied activities. KCCs sanctioned earlier continue under the old rules.

## Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS)

- Short-term crop and allied loans through KCC up to **₹3 lakh** are given at **7%** a year (MISS for 2025-26, RBI circular of 13 January 2026). The Union Budget 2025-26 announced raising this limit to ₹5 lakh – check the scheme notification for the current year.
- The Government gives lenders a **1.5%** subvention, and farmers who repay promptly get a further **3%** (Prompt Repayment Incentive) – an effective rate of **4%**.
- Within the ₹3 lakh, loans for animal husbandry and fisheries are covered up to ₹2 lakh.
- Small and marginal farmers with KCC can get the same rate for up to 6 months after harvest on loans against **negotiable warehouse receipts**.
- If a farmer has more than one KCC linked to the same land, the benefit goes only to the account with the highest sanctioned limit.

## Relief for natural calamities

- Applies when the State declares a calamity and the crop loss is **33% or more** (certified by the district authority).
- Existing crop loans can be **restructured** – repayable over up to **2 years** when the loss is 33%–50%, and up to **5 years** (including up to 1 year's moratorium) when the loss is 50% or more. Restructured accounts keep their standard classification.
- Fresh crop loans are given to affected farmers; KYC and collateral are relaxed.

## Differential Rate of Interest (DRI) scheme

- Started in **1972** for the poorest borrowers – loans at **4%** a year.
- **Eligibility:** annual family income up to **₹18,000** (rural) or **₹24,000** (urban and semi-urban) and small landholdings.
- **Loan:** up to **₹15,000** per beneficiary, plus up to **₹20,000** for housing.
- **Targets:** DRI lending of at least **1% of the previous year's total advances**; at least **40%** to SC/ST beneficiaries; at least two-thirds through rural and semi-urban branches.
- Repayment up to 5 years (with a grace period); security is hypothecation of the assets created.

## Self-Help Groups and DAY-NRLM

- **SHG–Bank Linkage Programme** started by NABARD in **1992**. An SHG has **10–20** members (minimum 5 in difficult and tribal areas, groups of persons with disabilities and similar special cases).
- **DAY-NRLM** (Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission) – launched as NRLM in June 2011, renamed in March 2016. Centre and States share costs **60:40** (90:10 for the North-East and hill States).
- SHGs get a **Revolving Fund** of ₹20,000–₹30,000 and a Community Investment Fund. An SHG should have existed for at least **6 months** and follow the "Panchsutras" (regular meetings, savings, inter-loaning, repayment, books of account).

| SHG credit (cash credit limit of at least ₹6 lakh for 3 years) | Drawing power |
|---|---|
| First year | 6 times the corpus or ₹1.5 lakh, whichever is higher |
| Second year | 8 times the corpus or ₹3 lakh, whichever is higher |
| Third year | At least ₹6 lakh, based on the micro credit plan |
| Fourth year onwards | Above ₹6 lakh, based on the micro credit plan and track record |

- **Security:** no collateral and no margin up to **₹10 lakh**; between ₹10 lakh and **₹20 lakh** no collateral and no lien on the group's savings account (a margin of up to 10% on the excess over ₹10 lakh may be taken). Such loans are covered under CGFMU.
- **Interest subvention:** women SHGs get loans up to ₹3 lakh at **7%**; prompt payers in notified districts get an additional 3% subvention. Indian Bank is the nodal bank for the subvention for 2025-26.

## Joint Liability Groups (JLGs)

- Informal groups of **4–10** people doing similar activities (often tenant farmers and oral lessees without land titles), who give a **mutual guarantee** for loans. Promoted by NABARD from 2006. Loans can be to the group or to members individually.

## Lead Bank Scheme

- Introduced in **1969** on the recommendations of the **Gadgil Study Group** and the **Nariman Committee**. A lead bank in each district coordinates credit planning.
- **District Consultative Committee (DCC):** chaired by the District Collector; the Lead District Manager (LDM) convenes it.
- **District Level Review Committee (DLRC):** chaired by the local Member of Parliament (Lok Sabha).
- **State Level Bankers' Committee (SLBC):** convened by the bank with the largest presence in the State.
- **Block Level Bankers' Committee (BLBC):** chaired by the LDM.
- **RSETIs** (Rural Self Employment Training Institutes) in each district, sponsored by lead banks, train rural youth for self-employment.

- **Negotiable warehouse receipts** are issued by warehouses registered with the **WDRA** (Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority, under the Warehousing Act, 2007); electronic NWRs (e-NWRs) are the norm. PSL limits for loans against them are in Chapter 11.

## Microfinance

- A **microfinance loan** is a collateral-free loan to a household with annual income up to **₹3 lakh**. Loan repayments of the household (including the new loan) cannot exceed **50%** of its monthly income.
- **NBFC-MFIs** must keep at least **60%** of total assets in microfinance loans (reduced from 75% in 2025); other NBFCs may hold up to **25%** of assets in microfinance loans (earlier 10%).
- No prepayment penalty; interest rates set by the lender's Board with a ceiling on spread; recovery agents may not call before **8 a.m. or after 7 p.m.** and must not use harassment.
- In February 2025, RBI cut the risk weight on microfinance loans of banks from 125% to 100%.

## Quick recap

- KCC 1998 (R.V. Gupta); no collateral up to ₹2 lakh; new 6-year KCC from 1 January 2027.
- MISS: 7% – 1.5% to banks – 3% PRI = 4% to the farmer; limit ₹3 lakh (₹5 lakh announced in Budget 2025-26).
- Calamity: loss of 33%+; restructure up to 2 years, or 5 years if loss ≥ 50%.
- DRI: 4%, ₹15,000 (+₹20,000 housing), 1% of previous year's advances, 40% SC/ST.
- SHG: 10–20 members; no collateral up to ₹20 lakh; JLG 4–10 members; Lead Bank Scheme 1969.

# Retail, Education, Housing and Social Security Schemes

## Education loans

### IBA Model Education Loan Scheme

| Point | Studies in India | Studies abroad |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum loan (indicative) | ₹10 lakh (higher for premier institutions) | ₹20 lakh |
| Margin | Nil up to ₹4 lakh; 5% above | Nil up to ₹4 lakh; 15% above |
| Security | Up to ₹4 lakh – none; ₹4–7.5 lakh – third-party guarantee; above ₹7.5 lakh – tangible collateral |

- **Moratorium:** course period plus 1 year; repayment up to 15 years after that. Simple interest during the moratorium; a concession if interest is paid during the course.
- Loans up to **₹7.5 lakh** covered under the **Credit Guarantee Fund for Education Loans (CGFEL)** do not need collateral or a third-party guarantee.
- Banks cannot reject an education loan application merely because of the location of the student's residence.

### Central support schemes

| Scheme | Benefit |
|---|---|
| **Central Sector Interest Subsidy (CSIS)** | Full interest subsidy during the moratorium for students with family income up to **₹4.5 lakh** a year, on loans up to ₹10 lakh for recognised courses in India |
| **PM-Vidyalaxmi** (November 2024) | Collateral-free, guarantor-free loans for students admitted to quality higher education institutions; **75% credit guarantee** on loans up to ₹7.5 lakh; **3% interest subvention** for family income up to ₹8 lakh on loans up to ₹10 lakh during the moratorium; single online portal |

## Housing

### PMAY-Urban 2.0 – Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS)

- For EWS (annual income up to **₹3 lakh**), LIG (**₹3–6 lakh**) and MIG (**₹6–9 lakh**) families without a pucca house anywhere in India, for loans sanctioned from 1 September 2024.
- Loan up to **₹25 lakh** for a house costing up to **₹35 lakh**, carpet area up to 120 sq m.
- **4% interest subsidy** on the first **₹8 lakh** of the loan for up to 12 years; maximum subsidy **₹1.80 lakh**, released in **5 yearly instalments**.
- Other verticals: beneficiary-led construction, affordable housing in partnership, affordable rental housing.

### Home loan rules

- **Loan-to-value (LTV):** up to **90%** for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for ₹30–75 lakh and 75% above ₹75 lakh. Stamp duty and registration can be included in the property cost for loans up to ₹10 lakh.
- **Risk weights** are lower for low-LTV loans; housing loans count as PSL within the limits in Chapter 11.

## PM SVANidhi (street vendors)

- Launched **June 2020**; restructured in 2025 with lending extended to **31 March 2030**.
- Working capital loans in three tranches: **₹15,000**, then **₹25,000**, then **₹50,000**, each after repaying the previous one.
- **7% interest subsidy** credited quarterly, cashback for digital transactions, and a UPI-linked **RuPay credit card** for vendors who repay the second loan. No collateral.

## Financial inclusion schemes

### Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

- Launched **28 August 2014** – one basic account for every household, now every adult.
- Zero balance BSBD account with a **RuPay debit card** carrying accident cover of **₹2 lakh** (₹1 lakh for accounts opened before 28 August 2018).
- **Overdraft** of up to **₹10,000** for eligible account holders (no conditions up to ₹2,000); age limit for overdraft 18–65 years. Covered under CGFMU.

### Social security schemes

| Scheme | Age | Premium | Cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PMJJBY** (life, 9 May 2015) | 18–50 (cover up to 55) | **₹436** a year | **₹2 lakh** on death from any cause; 30-day lien period for new enrolments (except accidental death) |
| **PMSBY** (accident, 9 May 2015) | 18–70 | **₹20** a year | **₹2 lakh** for accidental death or total permanent disability; ₹1 lakh for partial permanent disability |
| **APY** (pension, 9 May 2015) | Join at 18–40 | Monthly contribution based on age and pension chosen | Guaranteed pension of **₹1,000–₹5,000** a month from age 60; spouse gets the same pension and nominee the corpus. Income-tax payers cannot join since 1 October 2022. Continued till 2030-31 |

- The cover year for PMJJBY and PMSBY runs from **1 June to 31 May**, with auto-debit from the savings account.

### National Pension System (NPS)

- Regulated by **PFRDA**. Indian citizens, NRIs and OCIs aged **18–70** can join.
- **NPS Vatsalya** (launched 18 September 2024): for minors, opened by a parent or guardian; minimum **₹250** to open and per year, no maximum; default investment choice LC-50; partial withdrawal of up to 25% of own contributions after 3 years for education, illness or disability; at 18 the account can continue as regular NPS. On exit, up to 80% can be withdrawn as a lump sum and at least 20% must buy an annuity (the full corpus can be withdrawn if it is ₹8 lakh or less).

## Small savings schemes

Rates are set by the Ministry of Finance every quarter, linked to G-sec yields (Shyamala Gopinath Committee, 2011). For **October–December 2026** they are unchanged:

| Scheme | Rate | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Post Office savings | 4.0% | – |
| Public Provident Fund (PPF) | 7.1% | 15 years; ₹500 – ₹1.5 lakh a year; tax-free |
| Sukanya Samriddhi (SSY) | 8.2% | Girl child below 10; ₹250 – ₹1.5 lakh a year; deposits for 15 years; matures 21 years after opening; 50% withdrawal for education after 18 |
| Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS) | 8.2% | Age 60+ (55–60 for retirees under conditions); up to **₹30 lakh**; 5 years, extendable by 3; interest paid quarterly; TDS applies |
| National Savings Certificate (NSC) | 7.7% | 5 years |
| Kisan Vikas Patra | 7.5% | Doubles in 115 months |
| Monthly Income Scheme | 7.4% | Up to ₹9 lakh single, ₹15 lakh joint |

- **SCSS premature closure:** after 1 year with a 1.5% deduction (before 2 years) or 1% (after 2 years). Not available to HUFs and NRIs.
- **PMVVY** (LIC pension scheme for senior citizens) closed for new subscriptions after **31 March 2023**.
