# Banking and Financial Updates, 2025–26

Every item below was checked against the RBI or Government document. Each is grouped by subject so it is easy to revise.

## Regulation and supervision of banks

- **Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR) discontinued** from **18 May 2026** for commercial banks (and separately for SFBs, payments banks and co-operative banks). Banks already hold capital for market risk and value investments at market prices, so the extra buffer was no longer needed. The IFR balance is moved "below the line" to Statutory Reserve, General Reserve or the balance in P&L and counts as **Tier 1 capital**.
- **Dividend Directions, 2026** (issued 10 March 2026, apply from **FY 2026-27**): a bank can pay dividend only if it met the regulatory capital requirement in the previous year. The payout allowed depends on the bank's CET1 ratio band, with an overall ceiling of **75% of profit after tax** for commercial banks. Exceptional or extraordinary profits cannot be used for dividend. RRBs have separate directions.
- **Pillar 3 disclosures revised** (30 July 2026): each bank needs a Board-approved disclosure policy and a "Regulatory Disclosure Section" on its website; disclosures apply to unlisted banks too. The new quarterly, half-yearly and annual templates start from the periods ending **30 June 2027, 30 September 2027 and 31 March 2028** respectively.
- **Compliance function:** the Board has overall responsibility; the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) is chosen on the recommendation of a senior-level selection committee, should be **not more than 55 years** old at appointment, with **at least 15 years' experience** (5 of them in audit, finance, compliance, legal or risk), and gets a **minimum fixed tenure of 3 years**. The Board/Audit Committee reviews the function at least every quarter.
- **ECLGS 5.0** (Government scheme run by NCGTC, May 2026): RBI allowed a **0% risk weight on 75% of the guaranteed portion**, provided the claim is expected to be settled within 30 days of invocation (June 2026). The rest of the exposure carries the normal risk weight.
- **Capital market exposure – new limits from 1 July 2026:** loans to an individual against eligible securities are capped at **₹1 crore** (earlier ₹20 lakh) and loans for subscribing to IPOs, FPOs or ESOPs at **₹25 lakh** (earlier ₹10 lakh), both counted across the whole banking system. The borrower must bring at least **25% cash margin** for IPO funding. The date was deferred from 1 April 2026.
- **Expected Credit Loss (ECL) directions** (final, 27 April 2026): commercial banks move to forward-looking ECL provisioning with three stages and prudential floors from **1 April 2027**; NPA recognition rules are unchanged.
- **Basel III credit risk:** a new, more risk-sensitive standardised approach for credit risk also takes effect from **1 April 2027**.
- **Lending to REITs** allowed from 1 October 2026 – only listed, SEBI-registered REITs where at least 80% of the assets are completed and have earned positive cash flow for at least a year; the loan must be fully secured and cannot have bullet or balloon repayment.

## NBFCs

- **Upper Layer (NBFC-UL)** from 24 June 2026: any NBFC with asset size of **₹1 lakh crore and above** is placed in the Upper Layer (replacing the earlier scoring method). Government-owned NBFCs can now be in the Upper Layer but are exempt from the listing requirement; other NBFC-ULs must list within three years.
- **Infrastructure Finance Companies in the Upper Layer:** exposure limit for a group of connected counterparties raised from **35% to 45% of Tier 1 capital**.

## Foreign exchange and external sector

- **Swap facility (June 2026):** RBI offered banks a US dollar–rupee swap for fresh FCNR(B) deposits of 3–5 years and for ECBs/overseas foreign currency borrowings with average maturity of 3 years or more. Swap positions arising from these are **excluded from the NOP-INR limit** (8 June 2026).
- **CRR/SLR exemption:** fresh FCNR(B) deposits (3–5 years, from 8 June 2026) and fresh NRE term deposits (3 years or more, from 19 June 2026) were exempt, until the window closed on **31 August 2026**. Loans made against such deposits are excluded from ANBC (up to the exempt amount) when computing priority sector targets.
- **NOP-INR:** since 27 March 2026, AD Category-I banks must keep their net open rupee position in the onshore deliverable market within **USD 100 million**.
- **FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026** – notified 13 January 2026, in force **1 October 2026**. After the 22 September 2026 amendment, export proceeds must be realised within **9 months** of export (**12 months** if invoiced or settled in rupees).
- **Merchanting trade:** the foreign exchange outlay can now be up to **6 months** (earlier 4); the whole transaction must still be completed within 9 months.
- **EDPMS/IDPMS:** entries of up to **₹10 lakh** per bill can be closed by the AD bank on the exporter's or importer's declaration that the money has been received or paid.
- **Rupee loans to neighbours:** AD banks in India and their overseas branches may lend in INR to persons resident in **Bhutan, Nepal and Sri Lanka** for cross-border trade.
- **Niryat Protsahan** (Export Promotion Mission, from 2 January 2026): interest subvention of **2.75% a year** on rupee pre- and post-shipment credit for MSME exporters.

## Credit

- **Collateral-free MSE loans:** limit raised from ₹10 lakh to **₹20 lakh** for loans sanctioned or renewed on or after **1 April 2026**.
- **Kisan Credit Card Directions** – for KCC loans sanctioned from **1 January 2027**: a 6-year composite facility covering crop loans, allied activities and investment needs; crop seasons standardised at 12 months (short-duration) and 18 months (long-duration); no collateral up to ₹2 lakh. Existing KCCs continue under the old rules.
- **Co-lending Directions, 2025** (from 1 January 2026): extended beyond priority sector to all loans and to all banks, NBFCs (including HFCs) and AIFIs; each partner keeps at least 10% of every loan on its books.
- **TReDS Directions, 2026:** minimum net worth of a TReDS operator ₹25 crore (existing platforms must comply by 31 March 2028); financing on the platform is **without recourse** to the MSME seller; assignment of receivables is registered with CERSAI.
- **Priority sector clarifications:** housing-loan limits are decided by the population of the urban agglomeration or town as per **Census 2011** (Table A-04); villages not listed use the limits for centres below 10 lakh population. When buying PSL portfolios, banks may rely on the originator's auditor certificate plus their own sample checks.

## Customer protection and service

- **RB-Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026** (from **1 July 2026**, replacing the 2021 scheme). The Ombudsman can award up to **₹30 lakh** for consequential loss and up to **₹3 lakh** for loss of time, expenses, harassment and mental anguish. Details in Chapter 9.
- **BSBD accounts – revised rules from 1 April 2026:** no minimum balance; free cash deposits with no limit on number; free ATM-cum-debit card with no annual fee; cheque book of at least 25 leaves a year; internet and mobile banking; passbook or e-statement; at least **four free withdrawals a month** – digital payments (UPI, NEFT, IMPS, POS) are **not** counted as withdrawals. One BSBD account per customer across banks.
- **Small-value digital frauds** (directions of June 2026, effective **1 January 2027**): for losses up to ₹50,000, the customer can get compensation of up to 85% of the loss, capped at ₹25,000, once in a lifetime, subject to prompt reporting.
- **Deposit insurance:** cover stays ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, but from **1 April 2026** DICGC charges banks a **risk-based premium** instead of a flat 12 paise per ₹100.
- **KYC updation reminders:** banks must send at least three advance notices (one by letter) before the KYC due date and three reminders after it, and record them; to be in place by 1 January 2026.
- **ATMs:** 75% of ATMs to dispense ₹100/₹200 notes from at least one cassette by 30 September 2025, and 90% by 31 March 2026.
- **'.bank.in' domain:** exclusive internet domain for Indian banks to fight phishing; registrar is IDRBT (authorised by NIXI).
- **PRAVAAH** portal: since 1 May 2025 all applications for RBI licences, authorisations and approvals are filed only online through PRAVAAH. **Connect 2 Regulate** (April 2025) invites public suggestions on regulations.

## Laws and taxes

- **Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025:** "substantial interest" threshold raised from ₹5 lakh to **₹2 crore** (1 August 2025); up to **4 nominees** for deposits, lockers and safe-custody articles (1 November 2025); director tenure in co-operative banks raised from 8 to 10 years; PSBs can fix their statutory auditors' pay.
- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026** (assent 6 April 2026): adds a **creditor-initiated insolvency resolution process** in which the debtor stays in possession, and enables group and cross-border insolvency frameworks.
- **Income-tax Act, 2025** applies from **1 April 2026** (replacing the 1961 Act). TDS on interest is now under **Section 393(1)**; the thresholds are unchanged (₹50,000; ₹1 lakh for senior citizens).
- **GST 2.0** (56th GST Council, 3 September 2025; effective 22 September 2025): two main slabs of **5% and 18%**; the 12% and 28% slabs were removed for most items; a special **40%** rate applies to luxury and sin goods.
- **New criminal laws** (1 July 2024): Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam replaced the IPC, CrPC and Indian Evidence Act. The rule that a person not heard of for seven years is presumed dead continues under the new evidence law.

## Schemes and savings

- **PM-KISAN** continued for **2026-27 to 2030-31** with an outlay of about ₹3.15 lakh crore (Cabinet, 31 July 2026); ₹6,000 a year in three instalments.
- **Atal Pension Yojana** continued up to 2030-31 (Cabinet, 21 January 2026).
- **NPS Vatsalya** (for minors): minimum ₹250 to open and per year; no upper limit; default choice LC-50; partial withdrawal after 3 years.
- **PM SVANidhi** restructured: lending period extended to 31 March 2030 with loans of ₹15,000, ₹25,000 and ₹50,000.
- **Small savings rates for October–December 2026:** unchanged (PPF 7.1%, SSY 8.2%, SCSS 8.2%, NSC 7.7%, PO savings 4%).
- **Agri-Clinics and Agri-Business Centres (ACABC)** extended for 2025-26: 44% subsidy for women, SC/ST and applicants from the North-East and hill areas; 36% for others.

## Indices, committees and people

- **Financial Inclusion Index** (RBI): **70.0 for March 2026**, up from 67.0; all three sub-indices improved, mainly Usage. Weights: Access 35%, Usage 45%, Quality 20%.
- **Inflation target** retained at 4% ± 2% for April 2026 – March 2031 (25 March 2026).
- **Q-SAFE** (Quantum Secure and Adaptive Financial Ecosystem): 8-member RBI expert committee set up on 25 May 2026 under **Prof. Anil Prabhakar (IIT Madras)** to prepare a roadmap for quantum-safe finance.
- **FATF:** India's **Vivek Aggarwal** took office as FATF Vice-President on 1 July 2026 for one year (July 2026 – June 2027).

> **Watch out:** Many 2026 notes say India's FATF Vice-Presidency is a two-year term held by the Revenue Secretary, that the repo rate is 5.25%, and that export proceeds can be realised in 15 months. All three are now wrong – see the last chapter.

## Quick recap

- IFR gone from 18 May 2026; dividend cap 75% of PAT from FY 2026-27.
- Loans against shares ₹1 crore and IPO financing ₹25 lakh per person, system-wide, from 1 July 2026.
- NBFC-UL = assets of ₹1 lakh crore or more; UL-IFC group limit 45% of Tier 1.
- Export realisation 9 months (12 if in INR) from 1 October 2026; merchanting outlay 6 months.
- RB-IOS 2026: ₹30 lakh + ₹3 lakh; BSBD: 4 free withdrawals, digital payments not counted.
