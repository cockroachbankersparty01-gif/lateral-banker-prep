# Credit Appraisal and Financial Analysis

## Reading a borrower's balance sheet

| Liabilities | Assets |
|---|---|
| **Net worth** – share capital, reserves and surplus (less intangibles and accumulated losses = **tangible net worth**) | **Fixed assets** – land, buildings, plant and machinery (net of depreciation) |
| **Long-term liabilities** – term loans, debentures, long-term deposits (repayable after 12 months) | **Investments** and other **non-current assets** |
| **Current liabilities** – bank working capital borrowings, sundry creditors, provisions, instalments of term loans due within 12 months | **Current assets** – cash, receivables, inventory, advances (realisable within 12 months) |
| | **Intangible / fictitious assets** – goodwill, patents, preliminary expenses, deferred revenue expenditure |

- **Tangible net worth (TNW)** = paid-up capital + reserves − intangible assets − accumulated losses.
- **Net working capital (NWC)** = current assets − current liabilities = long-term funds − non-current assets. It shows the long-term money supporting current assets.
- **Working capital gap** = current assets − current liabilities other than bank borrowings.
- Unsecured loans from promoters that are subordinated to bank loans are often treated as **quasi-equity**.

## Key ratios

### Liquidity

| Ratio | Formula | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ current liabilities | 1.33 is the benchmark under Tandon's second method |
| Quick (acid-test) ratio | (Current assets − inventory − prepaid expenses) ÷ current liabilities | 1:1 is comfortable |
| Cash ratio | (Cash + marketable securities) ÷ current liabilities | Strictest test |

### Leverage and solvency

| Ratio | Formula | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Debt–equity | Long-term debt ÷ tangible net worth | Usually up to 2:1 for term loans |
| TOL/TNW | Total outside liabilities ÷ TNW | Lower is better; up to 3:1 generally accepted |
| Proprietary ratio | TNW ÷ total tangible assets | Owners' share in assets |
| Fixed assets coverage ratio (FACR) | Net fixed assets ÷ term loans | At least 1.33 is preferred |
| **DSCR** | (PAT + depreciation + interest on term loan) ÷ (interest on term loan + term loan instalment) | Average 1.5–2 is comfortable; below 1.25 is risky |
| Interest coverage | EBIT ÷ interest | Times interest earned |

### Activity (turnover)

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Inventory turnover | Cost of goods sold ÷ average inventory |
| Inventory holding (days) | Average inventory ÷ cost of goods sold × 365 |
| Debtors' collection period (days) | Average debtors ÷ credit sales × 365 |
| Creditors' payment period (days) | Average creditors ÷ credit purchases × 365 |
| Asset turnover | Sales ÷ total assets |

- **Operating cycle** = raw material holding + work-in-progress + finished goods holding + debtors' collection period − creditors' payment period.

### Profitability and market valuation

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross / net profit margin | Gross profit or net profit ÷ sales |
| Return on assets (ROA) | Net profit ÷ average total assets |
| Return on equity (ROE) | PAT ÷ average shareholders' funds |
| Return on capital employed (ROCE) | EBIT ÷ capital employed |
| Earnings per share (EPS) | (PAT − preference dividend) ÷ number of equity shares |
| Price–earnings (P/E) | Market price ÷ EPS |
| Book value per share | Net worth ÷ number of equity shares |
| Price to book | Market price ÷ book value per share |
| Dividend payout / yield | Dividend per share ÷ EPS / dividend per share ÷ market price |

- **Du Pont analysis:** ROE = net profit margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier (total assets ÷ equity).

### Break-even and leverage

- **Contribution** = sales − variable cost; **P/V ratio** = contribution ÷ sales.
- **Break-even point (units)** = fixed cost ÷ (selling price − variable cost per unit); **BEP (sales value)** = fixed cost ÷ P/V ratio.
- **Cash break-even** = (fixed cost − depreciation) ÷ contribution per unit.
- **Margin of safety** = actual sales − break-even sales.
- **Operating leverage** = contribution ÷ EBIT; **financial leverage** = EBIT ÷ EBT; **combined leverage** = contribution ÷ EBT.

> **Exam tip:** A worked DSCR example: PAT ₹30 lakh, depreciation ₹10 lakh, interest on term loan ₹10 lakh, instalment ₹15 lakh. DSCR = (30 + 10 + 10) ÷ (10 + 15) = 50 ÷ 25 = **2.0**.

## Other tools of analysis

- **Comparative statements** put two or more years side by side; **common-size statements** show each item as a percentage of total assets or sales; **trend analysis** uses a base year of 100.
- **Funds flow statement** – sources and uses of long-term funds and the change in working capital.
- **Cash flow statement** (AS 3 / Ind AS 7) – cash from **operating, investing and financing** activities, prepared by the direct or indirect method. A healthy firm generates positive operating cash flow.

## Assessing working capital

| Method | Who it suits | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Turnover method** (Nayak Committee, 1992) | MSEs with fund-based limits up to ₹5 crore | Need = 25% of projected turnover; bank finances at least 20%, borrower 5% |
| **MPBF – Tandon Committee (1974)** | Larger borrowers (now optional) | Method I: 75% of the WC gap. Method II: 75% of current assets − other current liabilities (current ratio 1.33). Method III: as method II after excluding core current assets |
| **Cash budget** | Seasonal industries (sugar, tea), contractors | Peak cash deficit financed |
| **Banks' own methods** | After the **Kannan Committee (1997)** | Banks free to choose; MPBF no longer mandatory |

- **Chore Committee (1979)** recommended reducing dependence on cash credit and introduced stricter discipline; the **Marathe Committee (1982)** replaced credit authorisation with the **Credit Monitoring Arrangement**.
- **Drawing power (DP)** = value of eligible current assets (stock and receivables, based on stock statements) minus margin. A CC account whose DP is based on stock statements older than **3 months** is irregular; if irregular for 90 days it becomes NPA.

### Loan system for delivery of bank credit

- For borrowers with aggregate fund-based working capital limits of **₹150 crore and above** from the banking system, at least **60%** of the limit must be drawn as a **working capital loan** (minimum tenor 7 days); only the rest can be cash credit.
- The **undrawn** portion of cash credit to such borrowers carries a credit conversion factor of **20%** for capital.

## Term loans and project appraisal

- **Appraisal covers:** technical feasibility, economic and commercial viability (demand, market), financial viability (cost of project, means of finance, profitability), and management.
- **Cost of project** = land, building, plant and machinery, preliminary and pre-operative expenses, contingencies and **margin money for working capital**.
- **Discounted cash flow tools:** NPV (accept if positive), IRR (accept if above the cost of capital), payback period, sensitivity analysis.
- For restructuring and large exposures, a **Techno-Economic Viability (TEV)** study is done.

## Pricing of loans

- New floating-rate loans to individuals and MSEs are linked to an **external benchmark** (EBLR) and reset at least once in 3 months; other loans may use MCLR.
- **Credit ratings:** banks use internal ratings and, for capital, ratings of RBI-accredited agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings, Acuité, Infomerics). Long-term ratings run from AAA (highest safety) to D (default); short-term from A1+ to D.

## Quick recap

- TNW = capital + reserves − intangibles − losses; NWC = CA − CL.
- DSCR = (PAT + dep. + interest) ÷ (interest + instalment); current ratio 1.33; quick ratio 1:1.
- BEP = fixed cost ÷ contribution per unit; P/V = contribution ÷ sales.
- Turnover method up to ₹5 crore: 25% / 20% / 5%; Tandon Method II gives current ratio 1.33.
- ₹150 crore+ borrowers: 60% loan component; undrawn CC CCF 20%.

# NPAs, Provisioning and Frauds

## When an account becomes NPA

| Facility | NPA when |
|---|---|
| Term loan | Interest or instalment **overdue for more than 90 days** |
| Cash credit / overdraft | Account **out of order** for more than 90 days |
| Bills purchased / discounted | Overdue for more than 90 days |
| Short-duration crops | Instalment or interest overdue for **two crop seasons** |
| Long-duration crops | Overdue for **one crop season** |
| Securitisation liquidity facility | Outstanding for more than 90 days |
| Derivatives | Overdue receivable (positive mark-to-market) for more than 90 days |

- **Out of order:** the balance stays above the limit or drawing power continuously for 90 days; or, when it is within the limit, there are no credits for 90 days, or credits are not enough to cover the interest debited in that period.
- A cash credit account is also treated as NPA if the **stock statements** are older than 3 months for 90 days, or the **limit is not reviewed** within 180 days of the due date.
- Classification is done as part of the **day-end process** for each date, and an NPA is **upgraded only when all arrears of interest and principal are paid** (RBI clarification of November 2021).

## Special Mention Accounts (early stress)

| SMA class | Loans other than revolving | Cash credit / overdraft |
|---|---|---|
| SMA-0 | Overdue up to 30 days | – |
| SMA-1 | Overdue 31–60 days | Out of order 31–60 days |
| SMA-2 | Overdue 61–90 days | Out of order 61–90 days |

- Banks report all borrowers with aggregate exposure of **₹5 crore and above** to RBI's **CRILC** (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits), including SMA status, and report defaulting large borrowers weekly.

## Asset classification and provisioning

| Category | Definition | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Performing | 0.40% generally; farm credit and SME 0.25%; commercial real estate (CRE) 1%; CRE–residential housing 0.75% |
| **Sub-standard** | NPA for up to 12 months | **15%**; unsecured exposures **25%** |
| **Doubtful – D1** | Doubtful up to 1 year | **25%** of the secured part + 100% of the unsecured part |
| **Doubtful – D2** | Doubtful 1–3 years | **40%** of the secured part + 100% unsecured |
| **Doubtful – D3** | Doubtful more than 3 years | **100%** |
| **Loss** | Loss identified by the bank, auditors or RBI, not yet written off | **100%** |

- Interest on an NPA is not taken to income until it is actually **received**; interest already booked but not received is reversed.
- **Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** = total provisions held (including technical write-offs) ÷ gross NPAs (including technical write-offs). A higher PCR means a bank is better protected.
- **Expected credit loss (ECL):** under the final directions of **27 April 2026**, commercial banks (other than SFBs, payments banks and RRBs) move from the "incurred loss" model above to an ECL model from **1 April 2027** (NPA recognition rules stay the same), classifying loans in three stages – Stage 1 (12-month expected loss), Stage 2 (significant increase in credit risk – lifetime loss) and Stage 3 (credit-impaired), with a transition period to absorb the impact.

> **Watch out:** NPA provisioning percentages above still apply until ECL begins on 1 April 2027 – they have not been withdrawn.

## Sale and transfer of stressed loans

- Under the **Transfer of Loan Exposures Directions (2021)**, banks can sell NPAs and SMA accounts to ARCs, other banks, NBFCs and AIFIs, usually through auctions (Swiss challenge allowed). If a bank sells at a price above the net book value, the excess can be taken to profit only on cash realisation.
- **Technical write-off** – the loan is removed from the books but recovery continues; **prudential write-off** is at head-office level.

## Frauds

### Governance (Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management, 15 July 2024)

- Board-approved fraud risk policy, reviewed at least once in **three years**.
- **Special Committee of the Board for Monitoring and Follow-up of Frauds (SCBMF)** – at least three Board members, including one whole-time director and at least two independent or non-executive directors; chaired by an independent director.
- **Natural justice:** before classifying an account as fraud, the bank must issue a detailed **show-cause notice** to the persons and entities concerned and give them at least **21 days** to reply, then pass a **reasoned order** (Supreme Court, *SBI v. Rajesh Agarwal*, March 2023).

### Early warning and red flags

- Banks need a data-analytics based **Early Warning Signals (EWS)** framework. An account with suspicious features is classified as a **Red Flagged Account (RFA)** and investigated (often by forensic audit).
- RFA status of accounts above the CRILC threshold is reported to RBI within **7 days**. The decision to classify as fraud or lift the red flag must be taken within **180 days**.

### Reporting

| To | What and when |
|---|---|
| RBI | Through the Fraud Monitoring Returns portal, within **14 days** of classifying a fraud |
| Law enforcement – private and foreign banks | Below ₹1 crore: State/UT police; ₹1 crore and above: State police and also the **Serious Fraud Investigation Office** (MCA) |
| Law enforcement – public sector banks | Below **₹6 crore**: State/UT police; ₹6 crore and above: **CBI** |

- **Central Fraud Registry (CFR)** – RBI's searchable database of reported frauds, used by banks when appraising credit.
- Frauds involving more than **₹50 crore** are referred to the **Advisory Board for Banking and Financial Frauds** (under the CVC) before staff accountability action in PSBs.
- Cash shortages above **₹10,000** (or above ₹5,000 if detected by management or auditors and not reported the same day) are reported as frauds.
- A fraud case up to **₹1 crore** can be closed for reporting purposes if the investigation or trial has been pending for more than 3 years from the FIR.

### Other safeguards

- **Legal audit** of title documents for loans of **₹5 crore and above** (₹1 crore for SFBs, LABs and RRBs), at sanction and periodically.
- **Penal measures:** a borrower classified as fraud (and its promoters and directors) is barred from bank finance for **5 years** from full repayment of the fraud amount.
- Banks must examine accounts for fraud before selling them to other lenders or ARCs.

## Quick recap

- NPA: 90 days overdue / out of order; agriculture 2 or 1 crop seasons; upgrade only after full arrears paid.
- SMA-1 31–60, SMA-2 61–90 days; CRILC for exposure ₹5 crore+.
- Provisions: standard 0.40%; sub-standard 15% (25% unsecured); D1/D2/D3 25/40/100% of secured part; loss 100%.
- ECL from 1 April 2027.
- Fraud: SCN with 21 days; RFA decision in 180 days; RBI within 14 days; PSB frauds ₹6 crore+ to CBI.
