# Securities, Charges and Documentation

## What makes good security

Adequacy of value, marketability, ease of finding its value, stability of value, clear title, ease of transfer, and freedom from contingent liabilities. **Primary security** is the asset created out of the loan (stock, machinery); **collateral security** is any additional security (a house, an FD, a guarantee).

## Modes of creating a charge

| Mode | Law | Possession | Typical assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lien** | Sec 171, Contract Act | With the bank | Securities, valuables in the bank's hands as banker |
| **Pledge** | Sec 172–179, Contract Act | With the lender | Gold, goods, NSC, shares (in demat form – pledge through the depository) |
| **Hypothecation** | Defined in Sec 2(n), SARFAESI Act | With the borrower | Stocks, book debts, vehicles, machinery |
| **Mortgage** | Sec 58, Transfer of Property Act | Usually with the borrower | Land, buildings |
| **Assignment** | Sec 130, TPA; Sec 38, Insurance Act | – | Book debts, LIC policies, supply bills, receivables |
| **Set-off** | Banking practice | – | Deposits against loans of the same customer |

### Pledge

- **Bailment** of goods as security for a debt (Sec 172). The pledgee can **sell after reasonable notice** if the pledgor defaults (Sec 176), or sue on the debt and keep the goods as collateral.
- A valid pledge can be made by a mercantile agent in possession of goods (Sec 178) and by a person holding goods under a voidable contract that has not been rescinded (Sec 178A).

### Hypothecation

- An equitable charge on movable property without transfer of possession. Because the bank does not hold the goods, it relies on stock statements, inspections, insurance and **drawing power** calculations.
- Enforced by taking possession under SARFAESI. For vehicles, the hypothecation is endorsed on the registration certificate with the RTO.

### Mortgage – six kinds (Sec 58, TPA)

| Kind | Possession | Personal liability | Remedy of mortgagee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple | With mortgagor | Yes | Suit for sale |
| By conditional sale | With mortgagor | No | Suit for foreclosure |
| Usufructuary | **With mortgagee** (takes rents and profits) | No | Keeps possession until repaid |
| English | Absolute transfer, re-transfer on repayment | Yes | Sale, including without court in some cases (Sec 69) |
| **By deposit of title deeds** (equitable) | With mortgagor | Yes | Suit for sale / SARFAESI |
| Anomalous | Any combination | As agreed | As agreed |

- **Equitable mortgage** (mortgage by deposit of title deeds, Sec 58(f)) is allowed only in towns **notified by the State Government**. It is the most common form for bank loans because no registration is needed unless the memorandum itself is the mortgage instrument (some States require filing).
- Other mortgages securing ₹100 or more must be registered (Registration Act, 1908) and need stamp duty.
- **Right of redemption** (Sec 60) – the mortgagor can redeem on paying the debt; any clause that prevents this ("clog on redemption") is void.
- **Second or puisne mortgage** – a later mortgage on the same property; **pari passu** charge – lenders share the security equally; **subservient** charge – ranks below the existing charges.

### Assignment

- Transfer of an **actionable claim** (book debts, receivables, insurance policies). A **legal assignment** is in writing and notice is given to the debtor; an equitable assignment lacks one of these. LIC policies are assigned under **Sec 38 of the Insurance Act**.

## Guarantee and indemnity

| Point | Indemnity (Sec 124) | Guarantee (Sec 126) |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | Two – indemnifier and indemnity holder | Three – creditor, principal debtor and surety |
| Liability | Primary | Secondary (arises on the debtor's default) |
| Purpose | Make good a loss | Pay the debt of another |

- **Sec 128** – the surety's liability is **co-extensive** with that of the principal debtor (unless the contract limits it).
- **Sec 129** – a **continuing guarantee** covers a series of transactions; it can be **revoked by notice** for future transactions (Sec 130) and ends on the surety's death for future transactions (Sec 131), unless the contract says otherwise.
- **Discharge of surety:** variation of the contract without his consent (Sec 133); release of the principal debtor (Sec 134); giving time to or compounding with the debtor (Sec 135); creditor's act that impairs the surety's remedy (Sec 139).
- **Rights of surety:** subrogation – on paying, he steps into the creditor's shoes (Sec 140); benefit of all the creditor's securities (Sec 141); indemnity from the principal debtor (Sec 145); co-sureties share equally (Sec 146).
- **Bank guarantees:** performance, financial and deferred-payment guarantees. Under the amended **Sec 28 of the Contract Act**, a bank guarantee may limit the time for making a claim, but the claim period must be **at least one year** from the end of the guarantee period.

## Registration of charges

| Where | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Registrar of Companies (Sec 77, Companies Act) | Charges created by companies | Within 30 days of creation |
| **CERSAI** (Sec 20–26, SARFAESI) | Security interest in immovable property, hypothecation, assignment of receivables, TReDS assignments | Within 30 days of creating the security interest |
| RTO | Hypothecation of vehicles | At registration |
| Depository | Pledge of demat securities | On creation |
| Information Utility (IBC) | Financial information of debts (NeSL) | As per IBBI rules |

- **CERSAI** – Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India – started on **31 March 2011**. Under Sec 26E of SARFAESI (2016), a secured creditor whose charge is registered with CERSAI has **priority over Government dues**, including taxes. CERSAI also runs CKYCR.

## Limitation Act, 1963

| Claim | Limitation period | Starts from |
|---|---|---|
| Money lent, demand promissory note, overdraft | **3 years** | Date of the loan / date of the note |
| Guarantee | 3 years | Date of demand on the guarantor (breach) |
| Money secured by a mortgage of immovable property | **12 years** | When the money becomes due |
| Foreclosure by mortgagee | 30 years | When the money becomes due |
| Execution of a decree | 12 years | Date of the decree |
| Suits by the Central or State Government | **30 years** | As per the article |

- **Sec 18** – a written, signed **acknowledgement** of the debt made **before** the period expires gives a fresh period from the date of acknowledgement.
- **Sec 19** – a **part-payment** made before expiry (shown in the debtor's handwriting or a signed writing) also gives a fresh period.
- **Sec 5** – courts can condone delay in appeals and applications for sufficient cause (not in suits).
- The Limitation Act applies to DRT applications and SARFAESI proceedings.

> **Exam tip:** Banks obtain a **balance confirmation / revival letter** before three years end – that is an acknowledgement under Sec 18. A document signed after the limitation period has expired is a fresh promise under Sec 25(3) of the Contract Act, not an acknowledgement.

## Stamp duty and documents

- Loan documents need stamp duty under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 or the State Stamp Act (stamp duty is a State subject for most instruments). An unstamped or under-stamped document is not admissible in evidence until the duty and penalty are paid. **E-stamping** is now widely used.
- Typical documents: demand promissory note, loan agreement, hypothecation deed, guarantee deed, memorandum of deposit of title deeds, letters of continuity and waiver, sanction letter and its acceptance.

## Quick recap

- Pledge – possession with lender; hypothecation – with borrower; mortgage – Sec 58 TPA, six kinds.
- Equitable mortgage only in notified towns; no registration needed.
- Guarantee – Sec 126; co-extensive liability (128); continuing guarantee (129); discharge (133–139).
- CERSAI within 30 days; ROC within 30 days; CERSAI charge beats Government dues (Sec 26E).
- Limitation: loans 3 years; mortgage 12 years; Government 30 years; Sec 18 acknowledgement, Sec 19 part-payment.

# Recovery, SARFAESI and Insolvency

## Recovery options for banks

| Route | Main features |
|---|---|
| Civil suit | Slow; decree executable for 12 years |
| **Lok Adalat** (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987) | Settlement by consent; the award is a **decree** of a civil court, final and **not appealable**; no court fee |
| **DRT** (Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993) | Claims of **₹20 lakh and above**; appeal to DRAT |
| **SARFAESI** (2002) | Enforcement of security without the court for secured NPA loans |
| **IBC** (2016) | Time-bound insolvency resolution |
| Compromise / one-time settlement | Under the Board-approved policy |
| Sale to ARCs | Transfer of stressed loans for security receipts or cash |

## SARFAESI Act, 2002

- **Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act** – in force from **21 June 2002**. It lets secured creditors enforce security without going to court and provides for ARCs and CERSAI.
- **Who can use it:** banks, financial institutions, HFCs, ARCs, and NBFCs with assets of **₹100 crore** or more for secured loans of **₹20 lakh** and above.
- **Conditions:** the account must be an **NPA** and the debt secured. Where there is more than one secured creditor, lenders holding at least **60%** in value must agree (Sec 13(9)).
- **Not applicable (Sec 31):** debts below **₹1 lakh**; cases where the amount due is **less than 20%** of principal and interest; **agricultural land**; pledges and liens; aircraft and ships; certain other securities.

### Timeline

| Step | Section | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Demand notice to borrower | 13(2) | Borrower has **60 days** to pay |
| Bank's reply to the borrower's objection | 13(3A) | Within **15 days** |
| Measures – take possession, take over management, appoint a manager, ask the borrower's debtors to pay the bank | 13(4) | After 60 days |
| Possession notice published in two newspapers (one in the local language) | Rules | After taking possession |
| Notice before sale of immovable property | Rule 8 | At least **30 days** |
| Help of the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate / District Magistrate to take possession | 14 | Order within **30 days** (up to 60 days) |
| Appeal by the borrower to **DRT** | 17 | Within **45 days** of the measure |
| Appeal to **DRAT** | 18 | Within **30 days**, after depositing **50%** of the debt (DRAT can reduce it to 25%) |

- The borrower can **redeem** the asset by paying all dues and costs **before the sale notice is published** (Sec 13(8), as amended in 2016).
- The secured creditor may itself bid at the auction (Sec 13(5A)).
- Sale is at not less than the reserve price fixed on the basis of a registered valuer's report.

## Debts Recovery Tribunals

- Set up under the **RDB Act, 1993** for claims of banks and FIs of **₹20 lakh and above** (raised from ₹10 lakh in 2018). The DRT should dispose of an application in **180 days**.
- The DRT also hears SARFAESI appeals and is the **adjudicating authority under IBC for individuals and partnership firms**.
- **Appeal** from DRT to **DRAT** within **30 days**, after depositing 50% of the amount due (reducible to 25%).

## Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)

- Registered with RBI under Sec 3 of SARFAESI; minimum net owned fund **₹300 crore** (raised from ₹100 crore in 2022).
- Buy stressed loans from banks, pay with cash and/or **security receipts (SRs)** issued to qualified buyers; the ARC (sponsor) must hold a minimum share of the SRs it issues.
- Resolve by restructuring, change of management, sale of assets or settlement.
- **NARCL** (National ARC, 2021) buys large stressed loans; **IDRCL** manages their resolution. Sale of stressed loans by banks follows RBI's **Transfer of Loan Exposures Directions** (2021).

## Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

- Regulator: **IBBI** (set up 1 October 2016).
- **Adjudicating authority:** **NCLT** for companies and LLPs (appeal to NCLAT, then the Supreme Court); **DRT** for individuals and partnership firms (appeal to DRAT).
- Provisions for **personal guarantors** of corporate debtors are in force since 1 December 2019.

### Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Minimum default | **₹1 crore** (since March 2020; was ₹1 lakh) |
| Who can apply | Financial creditor (Sec 7), operational creditor after a demand notice (Sec 8–9), the corporate debtor itself (Sec 10) |
| Admission | NCLT should decide within **14 days**; the 2026 amendment requires it to record reasons for any delay |
| Moratorium (Sec 14) | From admission – no suits, recovery or enforcement of security against the debtor |
| Management | Interim Resolution Professional, then the Resolution Professional, under the **Committee of Creditors** (financial creditors only) |
| Resolution plan | Approved by **66%** of the voting share of the CoC, then by NCLT |
| Time limit | **180 days**, extendable once by **90 days**; overall **330 days** including litigation |
| Failure | Liquidation (Sec 33) |

- **Sec 29A** bars certain persons from submitting a plan – for example, wilful defaulters and promoters of accounts that have been NPA for more than a year unless they clear the overdues.
- **Liquidation waterfall (Sec 53):** (1) insolvency and liquidation costs; (2) workmen's dues for 24 months and secured creditors who give up their security; (3) other employees' dues for 12 months; (4) unsecured financial creditors; (5) Government dues for 2 years and any unpaid balance of secured creditors; (6) remaining debts; (7) preference shareholders; (8) equity shareholders.

### Pre-packaged process for MSMEs (PPIRP, 2021)

- For corporate debtors that are **MSMEs**, with default of at least **₹10 lakh**.
- The debtor stays in control ("debtor in possession") and proposes a base resolution plan; at least **66%** of unrelated financial creditors must approve before filing.
- To be completed within **120 days** of admission.

### IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026

- Assent on **6 April 2026**. Adds a **Creditor-Initiated Insolvency Resolution Process** (new Chapter IV-A) in which financial creditors start the process out of court and the debtor stays in possession, and enables frameworks for **group insolvency** and **cross-border insolvency**.

## Prudential framework for resolution of stressed assets

- RBI directions of **7 June 2019** for commercial banks (except RRBs), AIFIs, SFBs and larger NBFCs.
- Lenders must review a borrower within **30 days** of default ("review period") and sign an **Inter-Creditor Agreement**. Decisions bind all if approved by lenders holding **75% by value and 60% by number**.
- The **resolution plan** must be implemented within **180 days** from the end of the review period.

| If the plan is not implemented | Additional provision |
|---|---|
| Within 180 days from the end of the review period | **20%** |
| Within 365 days from the start of the review period | Further **15%** (total 35%) |

- The extra provision is reversed – half when an insolvency application is filed and the rest when it is admitted, or fully when the plan is implemented.
- For exposure of **₹100 crore and above**, the plan needs an **independent credit evaluation** by credit rating agencies (one for ₹100–500 crore, two above ₹500 crore) with a rating of RP4 or better.
- A **restructured** standard account is downgraded to NPA and can be upgraded only after satisfactory performance during the "specified period".

## Wilful defaulters

- **Master Directions of 30 July 2024** (in force 28 October 2024). Apply to accounts with outstanding of **₹25 lakh and above**.
- **Wilful default:** the borrower defaults despite having the capacity to pay; diverts or siphons off funds; disposes of the security without the lender's knowledge; or does not use the money for the stated purpose.
- The bank must examine the account for wilful default within **6 months** of it becoming NPA. An **Identification Committee** issues a show-cause notice, giving **15 days** to reply and a personal hearing; a **Review Committee** headed by the MD/CEO confirms the order.
- **Penal measures:** no additional facilities; no restructuring; the borrower and its directors are barred from floating new ventures with bank finance for **5 years**; cannot be on the Board of any regulated entity; names reported to credit information companies.
- **Large defaulter:** outstanding of **₹1 crore and above** classified as doubtful or loss.
- **Non-cooperative borrower:** exposure of **₹5 crore and above** who wilfully avoids engaging with the lender; fresh loans to them need higher provisions.

## Compromise settlements and technical write-offs

- RBI framework of **8 June 2023**: every bank needs a Board-approved policy on compromise settlements and technical write-offs, with valuation-based pricing and an accountability system.
- Settlements are allowed even for accounts classified as **wilful defaulter or fraud**, without affecting criminal proceedings.
- After a compromise, there is a **cooling period** of at least **12 months** (for loans other than farm credit) before fresh exposure to the same borrower.

## Quick recap

- SARFAESI: NPA + secured; not below ₹1 lakh or below 20% dues or agricultural land; 60 days notice; DRT appeal 45 days; DRAT 30 days with 50% deposit.
- DRT claims ₹20 lakh+; Lok Adalat award is final.
- IBC: ₹1 crore default; 180 + 90 days, 330 overall; CoC 66%; NCLT for companies, DRT for individuals.
- PPIRP: MSMEs, ₹10 lakh, 120 days. 2026 amendment: creditor-initiated process.
- Stressed assets: 30-day review; ICA 75%/60%; 180 days; 20% + 15% extra provisions. Wilful defaulter ₹25 lakh; large defaulter ₹1 crore.
