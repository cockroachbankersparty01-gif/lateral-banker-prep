# Banker–Customer Relationship

## Who is a customer

- No statute defines "customer". Courts have held that a person becomes a customer when an **account** is opened in his name; the length of the relationship does not matter (*Commissioner of Taxation v. English, Scottish & Australian Bank*, 1920).
- A person who only buys a draft or cashes a cheque over the counter is not a customer in the legal sense, though KYC rules treat him as one for that transaction.

## Kinds of relationship

| Service | Relationship (bank – customer) |
|---|---|
| Deposits | Debtor – creditor |
| Loans and advances | Creditor – debtor |
| Safe custody of articles | Bailee – bailor (Sec 148, Contract Act) |
| Safe deposit locker | Lessor – lessee |
| Collecting cheques, standing instructions | Agent – principal |
| Currency chest | Bank is agent of RBI |
| Money held for a specific purpose | Trustee – beneficiary |
| Pledge / mortgage / hypothecation | Pledgee / mortgagee / hypothecatee |

> **Exam tip:** In a deposit, the bank does not hold "the customer's money" – it becomes the owner of the money and owes the customer an equal amount. That is why a depositor cannot claim interest from a failed bank as a trust beneficiary.

## Duty of secrecy

- Based on *Tournier v. National Provincial and Union Bank of England* (1924). A bank may disclose information only:
  1. under **compulsion of law** (courts, income-tax and GST authorities, RBI, investigating agencies, Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891);
  2. under a **duty to the public** (e.g., wilful defaulter lists);
  3. in the **bank's own interest** (e.g., suing the customer);
  4. with the customer's **express or implied consent**.
- Sharing credit data with credit information companies is required under the **CIC (Regulation) Act, 2005**.

## Banker's rights

| Point | General lien | Right of set-off | Appropriation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Law | Sec 171, Contract Act | Banking practice / equity | Sec 59–61, Contract Act |
| Meaning | Right to retain goods and securities that come into the bank's hands as banker, for a general balance | Combining a debit and a credit balance of the same customer | Deciding which debt a payment goes to |
| Notice | Not needed to retain; needed before sale | Should be given | – |

- **Lien** is treated as an **implied pledge**, so the bank can sell after reasonable notice. Lien is **not available** on articles in safe custody, documents given for a specific purpose, money held in trust, or securities left by mistake.
- **Set-off** needs: debts already due (not future or contingent), the same customer in the **same right** (an individual's credit cannot be set off against a firm's debt or a trust account), and amounts that are certain.
- **Appropriation:** the debtor chooses first (Sec 59); if he does not, the creditor chooses (Sec 60); if neither does, payments go to debts in order of time (Sec 61). In a running account, **Clayton's rule** applies – the first credit wipes out the first debit. That is why a guaranteed account is "ruled off" on the guarantor's death or notice and a new account opened.

## Garnishee and attachment orders

- **Garnishee order** – Sec 60 and **Order XXI Rule 46** of the Civil Procedure Code. A court first issues an **order nisi** (show cause) and then an **order absolute** (pay the money to the court).
- It attaches only the balance at the time the order is **received**, not future credits. The bank may first exercise its right of set-off.
- A joint account is attached only if the order is against **all** joint holders. Trust accounts and money held for a specific purpose are not attachable for the trustee's personal debts.
- Similar **attachment orders** can come from income-tax, GST and other authorities.

## Deceased accounts – what to do on notice of death

| Account | Action |
|---|---|
| Individual | Stop operations; pay the nominee, or legal heirs if there is no nomination |
| Joint – "either or survivor" / "former or survivor" | Pay the survivor (for "former or survivor", the latter gets the money only after the former's death) |
| Joint – operated jointly | Pay the survivors **together with** the legal heirs of the deceased |
| Partnership (credit balance) | Surviving partners can operate for winding up, on a fresh mandate |
| Partnership (debit balance) | Stop operations and rule off the account so that the deceased partner's estate stays liable (Clayton's rule) |
| Company (director dies) | No effect on the account; only the signatories change |
| Agent dies | Cheques he signed earlier are paid, as the principal is alive |
| Principal dies | The agency ends; cheques signed by the agent are returned |

- **Nominee** receives the money as a **trustee** of the legal heirs; payment to the nominee is a full discharge to the bank. Even a succession certificate in favour of legal heirs does not stop payment to a nominee – only a court order can.
- Banks settle claims within **15 days** of receiving the complete claim and pay compensation for delay. For claims without nomination, banks follow a simplified procedure (indemnity, affidavit) up to a Board-approved limit.
- **Missing person:** a person not heard of for **7 years** is presumed dead (Indian Evidence Act rule, now in the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023).

### Terms to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Will / codicil | Legal declaration of how a person's property is to be distributed / a later document that changes or adds to a will |
| Testate / intestate | Died leaving a will / without a will |
| Probate | Copy of the will certified by a competent court, granted to the executor |
| Executor | Person named in the will to carry it out |
| Letters of administration | Issued by a court when a person dies intestate (or the will names no executor) |
| Administrator | Person appointed by the court to manage the estate |
| Succession certificate | Issued by a civil court for debts and securities of the deceased (bank balances, shares) |

## Nomination

- Legal basis: Sec 45ZA–45ZF of the BR Act, the **Banking Companies (Nomination) Rules, 2025** and RBI's directions, effective **1 November 2025**.
- **Deposit accounts:** up to **4 nominees**, either **simultaneous** (each gets a fixed percentage, totalling 100%) or **successive** (the next nominee gets the money only if the earlier one is not alive).
- **Lockers and articles in safe custody:** **successive** nomination only, up to 4 nominees.
- Available to individuals, including joint account holders and sole proprietors – not for accounts held in a representative capacity.
- A **minor** can be a nominee; the account holder names an adult to receive the amount on the minor's behalf.
- Thumb-impression nominations need two witnesses. Banks must acknowledge a nomination and, with consent, show "Nomination Registered" and the nominee's name on the passbook.

## Quick recap

- Deposit = debtor–creditor; safe custody = bailee–bailor; locker = lessor–lessee.
- Tournier case: four exceptions to secrecy.
- Lien = implied pledge (Sec 171); no lien on safe custody items.
- Garnishee: Order XXI Rule 46 CPC; nisi then absolute; only present balance attached.
- Up to 4 nominees; lockers and safe custody – successive only; nominee is a trustee for legal heirs.

# Deposit Accounts and Types of Customers

## Deposit products

| Type | Key rules |
|---|---|
| Savings bank | Interest set by each bank (deregulated October 2011); calculated on **daily balance**, credited at least quarterly |
| Current account | No interest; opening governed by RBI rules for borrowers (see below) |
| Term deposit | Minimum tenor **7 days**; maximum 10 years; premature withdrawal penalty disclosed upfront |
| Recurring deposit | Fixed monthly instalments; penalty for delay as per bank policy |
| BSBD account | Zero balance; free basic services; revised rules from 1 April 2026 (Chapter 2) |
| Small account | Opened without full documents, with limits (Chapter 8) |

- **Bulk deposit:** a single rupee term deposit of **₹3 crore and above** for commercial banks and SFBs (₹1 crore and above for RRBs). Banks may pay differential rates on bulk deposits.
- **Current accounts of borrowers:** to keep a borrower's cash flows visible to its lenders, RBI limits who may open current accounts for borrowers with large bank exposure. Broadly, a bank with at least 10% of the banking system's exposure to the borrower may open them; other banks may open only **collection accounts** that pass the money on to a lending bank.
- Interest on a matured but unclaimed term deposit: savings rate or the contracted rate, whichever is lower.

## Joint accounts

| Point | "Either or survivor" | "Former or survivor" | Jointly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operation | Any one | Only the first holder while alive | All together |
| Stop payment | Any one holder can give | Any one | Any one |
| Closure, nomination, loan | All holders | All holders | All holders |
| On death of a holder | Survivor gets the balance | Latter gets it only after the former's death | Survivors with legal heirs of the deceased |

- Other mandates: "anyone or survivor(s)", "latter or survivor".
- A joint holder's personal debt cannot be set off against a joint account.

## Minors

- Age of majority is **18 years** (Indian Majority Act, 1875). A minor's contract is **void ab initio** (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903), so banks do not lend to minors or allow overdrafts.
- A minor of any age can open an account through a natural or legal guardian. A minor aged **10 years and above** may open and operate a savings or term deposit account **independently**, within limits set by the bank.
- On reaching majority, the bank takes fresh specimen signatures and operating instructions.
- **Natural guardians (Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956):** for a boy or unmarried girl – the **father, then the mother** (custody of a child below 5 years is normally with the mother); for an illegitimate child – the mother, then the father; for a married girl – the husband. Under Muslim law, the mother is not the natural guardian of a minor's property.
- A mother can open an account as guardian of her minor child (RBI, since 1976).
- A minor can be an agent and can endorse instruments, but is not personally liable. He can be **admitted to the benefits** of a partnership but cannot be a partner (Sec 30, Partnership Act); on attaining majority he must decide within 6 months whether to become a partner.

## Special types of individual customers

- **Illiterate person:** thumb impression attested in the presence of a bank officer; withdrawals in person with a photograph on record; the terms are explained to him.
- **Visually impaired person:** banks must offer all facilities – cheque book, ATM card, net banking, locker – without discrimination; withdrawals with a witness.
- **Pardanashin woman:** identified by a person known to the bank; signature attested.
- **Person of unsound mind:** stop operations on notice; the account is operated by a guardian appointed under the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 or by a court.
- **Married woman:** can open accounts and borrow in her own name; liable on her own property.

## Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)

- Not a legal entity; managed by the **Karta**. Since the 2005 amendment to the Hindu Succession Act, daughters are coparceners by birth, and courts have accepted the eldest daughter as Karta.
- Account opened with a **joint Hindu family letter** signed by the Karta and all adult coparceners.
- The Karta's liability is **unlimited**; coparceners are liable only to the extent of their share in family property.
- An HUF cannot be a partner in a firm. On the Karta's death the next senior coparcener becomes Karta; debit balances should be ruled off.

## Partnership firms and LLPs

- **Indian Partnership Act, 1932.** Registration is optional, but an unregistered firm cannot sue third parties to enforce a contract (Sec 69).
- Maximum **50 partners** (Companies Act, 2013, Sec 464 and Rules).
- Partners are **jointly and severally liable** (Sec 25). Implied authority (Sec 19) does **not** include opening a bank account in a partner's own name for the firm, compromising a claim, submitting to arbitration, transferring immovable property or entering into a partnership.
- Account is opened with a **partnership letter/mandate** signed by all partners; it changes on the death, retirement or insolvency of a partner.
- **LLP (LLP Act, 2008):** a body corporate with perpetual succession; at least 2 designated partners, one of them resident in India; partners' liability limited to their agreed contribution; no maximum number of partners.

## Companies (Companies Act, 2013)

| Type | Members | Directors |
|---|---|---|
| Private company | 2 – 200 | At least 2 |
| Public company | At least 7 | At least 3 |
| One Person Company | 1 (with a nominee) | At least 1 |
| Section 8 company | Non-profit; licence from Central Government | As per type |

- A company can have up to **15 directors** (more with a special resolution). At least one director must have stayed in India for 182 days or more in the previous year. Listed companies need at least one-third independent directors; listed companies and large public companies need a woman director.
- **Documents for an account:** Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, Board resolution, PAN, list of authorised signatories and KYC of **beneficial owners**.
- **Memorandum of Association** – the charter (name, registered office, objects, liability, capital); acts beyond it are *ultra vires* and void (*Ashbury Railway Carriage v. Riche*, 1875). **Articles of Association** – internal rules.
- **Doctrine of indoor management** (*Royal British Bank v. Turquand*, 1856): an outsider dealing in good faith may assume internal formalities have been followed.
- **Borrowing:** the Board can borrow (Sec 179), but borrowing beyond paid-up capital plus free reserves plus securities premium needs a **special resolution** (Sec 180(1)(c)).
- **Charges** must be registered with the Registrar of Companies within **30 days** of creation (Sec 77); late registration is allowed with additional fees within the further time the law permits. An unregistered charge is void against the liquidator and other creditors.

## Other customers

- **Trusts (Indian Trusts Act, 1882):** operated by all trustees jointly unless the trust deed allows otherwise – a trustee cannot delegate (Sec 47). Obtain a copy of the trust deed.
- **Clubs, societies, associations:** registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 or as co-operatives; account opened on a resolution as per bye-laws.
- **Executors and administrators:** on production of probate or letters of administration; all must sign unless the will says otherwise.
- **Liquidator:** appointed by the court or NCLT; operates the company's account for winding up.

## Deposit insurance (DICGC)

- **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation**, a wholly owned subsidiary of RBI, under the DICGC Act, 1961 (formed 1978 by merging DIC with CGCI).
- Covers all commercial banks (including foreign bank branches, SFBs, payments banks, LABs, RRBs) and co-operative banks. Primary agricultural credit societies are not covered.
- **Cover: ₹5 lakh** per depositor per bank, principal and interest together, in the same right and capacity (from 4 February 2020; ₹1 lakh before that).
- **Not covered:** deposits of foreign governments, Central and State Governments, inter-bank deposits, and amounts due outside India.
- The **premium is paid by the bank**, not the depositor – half-yearly. It was a flat 12 paise per ₹100 of assessable deposits; from **1 April 2026** it is **risk-based**.
- **DICGC (Amendment) Act, 2021:** if a bank is placed under restrictions, depositors get up to ₹5 lakh within **90 days** (45 days for the bank to collect claims, 45 days for DICGC to verify and pay).

## Quick recap

- Term deposit minimum 7 days; bulk deposit ₹3 crore (₹1 crore for RRBs); SB interest on daily balance.
- Minor 10+ can operate independently; no loans to minors.
- Karta – unlimited liability; HUF cannot be a partner.
- Partners – joint and several liability; max 50; unregistered firm cannot sue (Sec 69).
- Company charge – ROC within 30 days; DICGC ₹5 lakh, premium by bank, risk-based from 1 April 2026.

# KYC and Anti-Money Laundering

## Legal framework

- **Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002** and the **PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005**.
- RBI's KYC rules, issued under Sec 35A of the BR Act, are now in the entity-wise **Know Your Customer Directions, 2025** (they replaced the 2016 Master Direction).
- Four pillars of KYC policy: **Customer Acceptance Policy, Risk Management, Customer Identification Procedure and Monitoring of Transactions.**
- Money laundering has three stages: **placement, layering and integration**.

## Customer due diligence (CDD)

- Identify the customer and the **beneficial owner** (BO) – the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the customer.

| Customer | Beneficial owner threshold |
|---|---|
| Company | More than **10%** of shares, capital or profits, or control |
| Partnership firm | More than **10%** of capital or profits |
| Unincorporated association / body of individuals | More than **15%** |
| Trust | Author, trustees, beneficiaries with 10% or more interest, and anyone exercising ultimate control |

- For individuals, obtain one **Officially Valid Document (OVD)** for identity and address, a recent photograph, and **PAN or Form 60**.
- **OVDs (6):** passport, driving licence, **proof of possession of Aadhaar number**, voter ID card, NREGA job card signed by a State Government officer, letter of the National Population Register with name and address.
- **Deemed OVDs** (only for address, when the OVD lacks the current address): utility bill not more than 2 months old, property or municipal tax receipt, pension payment order, letter of allotment of accommodation from specified employers. The customer must then submit an OVD with the current address within 3 months.
- **Aadhaar:** banks can use biometric or OTP-based e-KYC (Sec 11A of PMLA) and offline verification (Aadhaar Secure QR or XML not older than 3 days).
- **Accounts opened by OTP-based e-KYC (non-face-to-face):** aggregate balance up to ₹1 lakh and total credits up to ₹2 lakh a year; full CDD to be completed within a year; loans only up to ₹60,000.

## Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP)

- Treated as equal to face-to-face verification. Conducted live by a trained official of the bank, with the customer's informed consent.
- The video is **geo-tagged** with the customer's live location and time-stamped; connections from IP addresses outside India are blocked.
- Questions are varied to confirm **liveness** and rule out pre-recording; any prompting leads to rejection.
- The officer captures a photograph and obtains identity details through Aadhaar OTP e-KYC, offline Aadhaar verification or digital document checks. Accounts opened by V-CIP become operational only after a **concurrent audit** check.

## Small accounts

- Opened with a self-attested photograph and signature or thumb impression in the bank officer's presence.
- Limits: balance at any time **₹50,000**; total credits in a year **₹1 lakh**; withdrawals in a month **₹10,000**.
- Valid for **12 months**, extendable by another 12 months if the customer shows he has applied for an OVD. Foreign remittances cannot be credited until full KYC is done.

## Risk categorisation and updating KYC

| Risk | KYC updation at least every |
|---|---|
| High | 2 years |
| Medium | 8 years |
| Low | 10 years |

- If there is no change, a **self-declaration** through e-mail, mobile, ATM, net banking or letter is enough. For a change of address, the customer can give a self-declaration and the bank verifies it within two months.
- Banks must send **three advance notices** (one by letter) before the due date and **three reminders** after it (in force from 1 January 2026). Business correspondents may help customers update KYC.
- If KYC is not updated, the bank can impose **partial freezing** after due notice – credits allowed, debits stopped – and later freeze the account fully.
- **Politically exposed persons (PEPs)** – individuals entrusted with prominent public functions by a foreign country – need senior management approval and enhanced monitoring.
- **Non-profit organisations** must be registered on NITI Aayog's **DARPAN** portal; their records are kept for 5 years after the relationship ends.
- Banks must not have correspondent relationships with **shell banks** (banks with no physical presence in any country and not part of a regulated group).

## Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR)

- Run by **CERSAI**. Each customer gets a **14-digit KYC Identifier (KIN)**.
- Banks upload KYC records of new customers – individuals since 1 January 2017 and legal entities since 1 April 2021 – and update them when the customer gives new details.
- A bank that downloads valid, current records from CKYCR need not re-verify identity and address, but remains responsible for risk profiling, monitoring and periodic updation.

## Reporting to FIU-IND

FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit – India, under the Department of Revenue, set up 2004) receives reports through the bank's **Principal Officer**. A **Designated Director** ensures overall compliance.

| Report | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| CTR – cash transaction report | Cash transactions above **₹10 lakh**, or connected cash transactions in a month that together exceed ₹10 lakh | By the **15th** of the next month |
| STR – suspicious transaction report | Any suspicious transaction, attempted or completed, of any amount | Within **7 working days** of concluding it is suspicious |
| CCR – counterfeit currency report | Cash transactions where forged notes were found | By the 15th of the next month |
| NTR – non-profit transaction report | Receipts of NPOs above ₹10 lakh | By the 15th of the next month |
| CBWTR – cross-border wire transfer report | Cross-border wire transfers above ₹5 lakh | By the 15th of the next month |

- Records of transactions and identity are kept for at least **5 years** after the transaction or the end of the relationship.
- **Tipping off** the customer about an STR is prohibited.
- Accounts of persons on **UN Security Council sanctions lists** or notified under **Sec 51A of UAPA** are frozen without delay and reported.

## PAN and cash rules

| Transaction | Rule |
|---|---|
| Opening an account (other than BSBD) | PAN or Form 60 |
| Cash deposit | PAN if more than ₹50,000 in a day; PAN or Aadhaar if total cash deposits or withdrawals reach ₹20 lakh in a year |
| Time deposits | PAN if a deposit exceeds ₹50,000 or deposits total more than ₹5 lakh in a year |
| Drafts, pay orders, banker's cheques | Cash only below ₹50,000; ₹50,000 and above by debit to account or cheque |
| Foreign currency | Up to ₹50,000 can be bought for cash |
| Repayment of deposits | No cash repayment of ₹20,000 or more (earlier Sec 269T) |

- **FATCA/CRS:** banks collect a self-certification of tax residence from customers and report accounts of foreign tax residents to the Income-tax Department each year.

## Quick recap

- 6 OVDs; PAN or Form 60 compulsory; beneficial owner 10% (companies and firms).
- Small account: ₹50,000 balance, ₹1 lakh credits a year, ₹10,000 withdrawals a month, 12 + 12 months.
- KYC updation: 2/8/10 years; three notices and three reminders.
- CKYCR by CERSAI – 14-digit KIN.
- CTR above ₹10 lakh by the 15th; STR within 7 working days; records 5 years.
