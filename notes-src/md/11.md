# Basel III and Capital Adequacy

## The Basel accords

| Accord | Year | Main points | In India |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basel I | 1988 | Minimum 8% capital to risk-weighted assets, for credit risk; market risk added in 1996 | From 1992 (Narasimham Committee I); CRAR 9% from 1999-2000 |
| Basel II | 2004 | Three pillars; capital for operational risk; ratings-based risk weights | 2008-09 (foreign banks and banks with overseas branches from March 2008; others from March 2009) |
| Basel III | 2010 | Better-quality capital (CET1), buffers, leverage ratio, LCR and NSFR | Phased from 1 April 2013; fully in place from 1 October 2021 |

- The **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS)** was set up in 1974 and is hosted by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), Basel.

## Three pillars

1. **Pillar 1 – Minimum capital** for credit risk, market risk and operational risk.
2. **Pillar 2 – Supervisory review:** each bank assesses its own capital needs for all risks through an **ICAAP**; RBI reviews it through the **Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP)**.
3. **Pillar 3 – Market discipline:** public disclosures of capital, risk exposures and risk management (revised framework issued 30 July 2026).

## Components of capital

| Tier | What it includes |
|---|---|
| **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)** | Paid-up equity capital, share premium, statutory reserves, capital and other disclosed free reserves, balance in P&L, revaluation reserves (at a discount), **less** regulatory deductions (goodwill, intangibles, deferred tax assets, etc.) |
| **Additional Tier 1 (AT1)** | Perpetual non-cumulative preference shares (PNCPS) and **perpetual debt instruments** – no maturity, discretionary coupons, call only after 5 years with RBI approval; must be written down or converted if CET1 falls below the trigger or at the point of non-viability |
| **Tier 2** | General provisions and loss reserves (up to **1.25%** of credit risk-weighted assets), **subordinated debt** with at least **5 years'** maturity (amortised by 20% a year in the last 5 years), revaluation reserves at a discount |

> **2026 update:** The Investment Fluctuation Reserve was discontinued from **18 May 2026**; the balance is transferred to statutory or general reserve (or P&L) and counts as **Tier 1** capital.

## Minimum requirements in India

| Ratio | Minimum |
|---|---|
| CET1 | **5.5%** of RWAs |
| Tier 1 (CET1 + AT1) | **7%** |
| Total capital (CRAR) | **9%** |
| Capital Conservation Buffer (in CET1) | **2.5%** – so CET1 + CCB = 8% and total = **11.5%** |
| Countercyclical buffer (CCyB) | 0–2.5% of RWAs; framework since 2015, **not activated** so far |
| D-SIB surcharge (in CET1) | **SBI 0.80%, HDFC Bank 0.40%, ICICI Bank 0.20%** |
| Leverage ratio (Tier 1 ÷ exposure measure) | **4%** for D-SIBs, **3.5%** for other banks |

- Small finance banks and payments banks must keep a minimum CRAR of **15%**.
- If a bank's CET1 falls into the buffer range, it can still operate but must **restrict dividend payouts** until the buffer is rebuilt.

## Risk-weighted assets

### Credit risk – standardised approach

| Exposure | Risk weight |
|---|---|
| Cash, gold, claims on Government of India and RBI, State Government securities | 0% |
| Portion guaranteed by CGTMSE, CRGFTLIH, NCGTC (and 75% of the ECLGS 5.0 guaranteed part, if claims settle within 30 days) | 0% |
| Claims on banks | By the bank's CRAR / rating |
| Corporates | By external rating – AAA 20%, AA 30%, A 50%, BBB 100%, BB and below 150%; unrated 100% (higher for large unrated exposures) |
| Regulatory retail (individuals and small businesses, up to **₹7.5 crore** per counterparty) | 75% |
| Consumer credit (personal loans and similar, excluding housing, education, vehicle and gold loans) | 125% (credit card receivables 150%) |
| Microfinance loans | 100% (cut from 125% in February 2025) |
| Housing loans | 20%–50%, depending on loan size and loan-to-value ratio |
| Commercial real estate (CRE) | 100%; CRE–residential housing 75% |
| Capital market exposure | 125% |
| Venture capital funds | 150% |
| Unsecured part of NPAs | 150% if specific provisions are below 20%; 100% if 20–50%; 50% if 50% or more |

- **Off-balance-sheet items** are converted using credit conversion factors (CCF): e.g., direct credit substitutes 100%, performance guarantees 50%, short-term self-liquidating trade LCs 20%.
- RBI accepts ratings of six accredited agencies: **CARE, CRISIL, ICRA, India Ratings, Acuité and Infomerics**.

> **2026 update:** RBI has issued the final **Basel III standardised approach for credit risk** (Capital Charge for Credit Risk Directions, 2026), effective **1 April 2027** for commercial banks other than SFBs, payments banks and LABs, with more granular, risk-sensitive risk weights. The table above applies until then.

### Market and operational risk

- **Market risk** – capital for interest rate, equity, forex and commodity risk in the trading book, under the standardised approach.
- **Operational risk** – RBI issued directions for the Basel III **Standardised Approach** (based on a Business Indicator) in June 2023; until it takes effect, banks follow the **Basic Indicator Approach** – capital of 15% of average positive gross income of the last three years.
- **Operational risk event types (Basel):** internal fraud; external fraud; employment practices and workplace safety; clients, products and business practices; damage to physical assets; business disruption and system failures; execution, delivery and process management. Legal risk is included; strategic and reputational risks are not.

## Dividend and profit retention

- **Dividend Directions, 2026** (from FY 2026-27): eligibility depends on meeting capital requirements; the payout allowed rises with the CET1 ratio band, with an overall cap of **75% of PAT** for commercial banks.
- Sec 17 of the BR Act: at least **20%** of profit to the statutory reserve before dividend.

## Quick recap

- CET1 5.5%, Tier 1 7%, CRAR 9%, CCB 2.5% → 11.5%; CCyB not activated.
- D-SIBs: SBI 0.80%, HDFC Bank 0.40%, ICICI Bank 0.20%; leverage 4% (D-SIBs) / 3.5%.
- AT1 – perpetual; Tier 2 sub-debt ≥ 5 years; general provisions ≤ 1.25% of credit RWAs.
- Regulatory retail ₹7.5 crore at 75%; consumer credit 125%; microfinance 100%; CRE 100%.
- New Basel III credit-risk standardised approach from 1 April 2027; IFR removed from 18 May 2026.

# Liquidity, ALM and Money Markets

## Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)

- **LCR = Stock of High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) ÷ total net cash outflows over the next 30 calendar days**, to be at least **100%**. Phased in India from 1 January 2015 and at 100% since 1 January 2019.

| HQLA level | Examples | Haircut / cap |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Cash, excess CRR, G-secs above the SLR requirement, and a part of SLR securities allowed by RBI | No haircut, no cap |
| Level 2A | High-rated corporate bonds, PSE securities | 15% haircut |
| Level 2B | Lower-rated corporate bonds, certain listed equities | 50% haircut; Level 2 together at most 40% of HQLA (2B at most 15%) |

- **Run-off rates:** stable retail deposits 5%, less stable 10% (higher for wholesale and unsecured funding).

> **2026 update:** From **1 April 2026**, retail and small-business deposits that have internet or mobile banking access carry an additional **2.5%** run-off – i.e., **7.5%** for stable and **12.5%** for less stable deposits – because such money can leave faster.

## Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)

- **NSFR = Available Stable Funding ÷ Required Stable Funding**, at least **100%**, over a one-year horizon. In force in India from **1 October 2021**.

## Asset–Liability Management (ALM)

- RBI's ALM guidelines (1999, revised 2012) require an **ALCO** (Asset–Liability Committee) and statements of structural liquidity and interest-rate sensitivity.
- **Time buckets** for liquidity: next day, 2–7 days, 8–14 days, 15–30 days, 31 days–2 months, 2–3 months, 3–6 months, 6 months–1 year, 1–3 years, 3–5 years, over 5 years.

| Bucket | Maximum negative mismatch (as % of cumulative outflows) |
|---|---|
| Next day | 5% |
| 2–7 days | 10% |
| 8–14 days | 15% |
| 15–30 days | 20% |

- **Interest rate risk:** measured by **traditional gap analysis** (rate-sensitive assets minus rate-sensitive liabilities – effect on earnings) and **duration gap analysis** (effect on the economic value of equity).
- A positive gap (RSA > RSL) gains when rates rise; a negative gap gains when rates fall.

## Money market

| Segment | Tenor |
|---|---|
| Call money | Overnight |
| Notice money | 2 to 14 days |
| Term money | 15 days to 1 year |

- Call/notice/term money is unsecured, among banks and primary dealers. The **weighted average call rate** is RBI's operating target.
- **Collateralised markets:** market repo and **TREPS** (tri-party repo on CCIL's platform) – now larger than the call market.

| Instrument | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Treasury bills** | 91, 182 and 364 days; issued at a discount by RBI for the Government through auctions; minimum ₹25,000 |
| **Cash Management Bills** | Less than 91 days, for temporary Government cash mismatches |
| **Commercial Paper (CP)** | Issued by companies, NBFCs and AIFIs; **7 days to 1 year**; minimum **₹5 lakh** and multiples; must be rated; issued at a discount in demat form |
| **Certificate of Deposit (CD)** | Issued by banks (**7 days to 1 year**) and AIFIs (**1 to 3 years**); minimum **₹5 lakh** and multiples; in demat form; **CRR and SLR apply**; no grace period at maturity; the issuer may buy back after 7 days on equal terms to all holders |
| **Repo / reverse repo** | Short-term borrowing against securities with an agreement to repurchase |

- **Benchmarks:** FBIL publishes the overnight **MIBOR**, T-bill and other benchmarks; after LIBOR ended in 2023, foreign currency loans use alternative reference rates such as **SOFR**, and MIFOR was replaced by **Modified MIFOR**.
- **RBI Retail Direct** (November 2021) lets individuals buy G-secs directly. Indian G-secs are now in global bond indices (J.P. Morgan from June 2024, followed by Bloomberg and FTSE).

## Quick recap

- LCR = HQLA ÷ 30-day net outflows ≥ 100%; Level 2B haircut 50%; extra 2.5% run-off for digitally accessible deposits from 1 April 2026.
- NSFR ≥ 100% from 1 October 2021.
- ALM negative mismatch caps: 5%, 10%, 15%, 20% for the first four buckets.
- Call 1 day; notice 2–14 days; term 15 days–1 year.
- CP and CD: minimum ₹5 lakh; CD by banks 7 days–1 year, AIFIs 1–3 years; CRR/SLR on CDs.

# Exposure Norms, Supervision and Risk Management

## Large Exposures Framework (LEF)

- In force from **1 April 2019**. Limits are on **Tier 1 capital** (the eligible capital base).

| Exposure to | Limit |
|---|---|
| Single counterparty | **20%** of Tier 1 (up to 25% with Board approval in exceptional cases) |
| Group of connected counterparties | **25%** of Tier 1 |
| A "large exposure" | Any exposure of **10%** or more of Tier 1 – must be reported |

- Exposures to the Government of India, RBI and those guaranteed by them are exempt, as are certain inter-bank exposures.
- **NBFC Upper Layer IFCs** may take exposure of up to **45%** of Tier 1 to a group of connected counterparties (from 24 June 2026).

## Capital market exposure (CME)

- Total CME of a bank, on a solo and consolidated basis, cannot exceed **40%** of its eligible capital base; **direct** investment exposure within that cannot exceed **20%**.
- From **1 July 2026**, loans to an individual against eligible securities are capped at **₹1 crore**, and loans for IPOs, FPOs or ESOPs at **₹25 lakh**, both across the banking system, with at least 25% cash margin for IPO funding.
- A bank cannot hold more than **30%** of a company's paid-up capital (Sec 19(2), BR Act).

## Prompt Corrective Action (PCA)

- Revised framework in force from **1 January 2022** for commercial banks (not RRBs, SFBs or payments banks); separate PCA frameworks now apply to Government NBFCs and UCBs.

| Parameter | Risk threshold 1 | Threshold 2 | Threshold 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRAR (minimum incl. CCB 11.5%) | Up to 250 bps below | 250–400 bps below | More than 400 bps below |
| CET1 (minimum incl. CCB 8%) | Up to 162.5 bps below | 162.5–312.5 bps below | More than 312.5 bps below |
| Net NPA ratio | 6% to below 9% | 9% to below 12% | 12% and above |
| Tier 1 leverage ratio | Up to 50 bps below the minimum | 50–100 bps below | More than 100 bps below |

- **Mandatory actions:** at threshold 1, restrictions on dividends and on remittance of profits, and promoters/owners asked to bring in capital; threshold 2 adds limits on **branch expansion**; threshold 3 adds limits on **capital expenditure**. RBI may also take **discretionary actions** (restrict lending, deposits, new business, management changes).
- **Profitability** is monitored but is not a trigger.
- A bank exits PCA after **four continuous quarters** of compliance (including one audited annual statement) and RBI's supervisory comfort. A breach of threshold 3 can lead to amalgamation, reconstruction or winding up.

## Supervision

- **Board for Financial Supervision (BFS)**, 1994; a unified **Department of Supervision** since 2019.
- RBI rates banks under **risk-based supervision (SPARC)** – Supervisory Program for Assessment of Risk and Capital. Earlier ratings: **CAMELS** for Indian banks (Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Systems and control / Sensitivity) and **CALCS** for foreign banks (Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Liquidity, Compliance, Systems).
- **Supervisory Data Quality Index (sDQI)** measures the accuracy, timeliness, completeness and consistency of banks' returns to RBI.

## Internal control: three lines of defence

1. **Business units** own and manage their risks.
2. **Risk management and compliance** functions oversee them – the **Chief Risk Officer** (independent, fixed tenure, reports to the MD/CEO and the Board's risk committee) and the **Chief Compliance Officer** (Chapter 2).
3. **Internal audit** gives independent assurance – banks follow **Risk-Based Internal Audit**; **concurrent audit** covers a Board-decided share of the business on a near real-time basis.

## Types of risk in banking

| Risk | Meaning | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| Credit | Borrower or counterparty fails to pay | Credit rating, limits, collateral, guarantees, credit derivatives |
| Market | Loss from changes in interest rates, exchange rates, equity and commodity prices | Limits, VaR, duration, hedging |
| Liquidity | Inability to meet obligations when due | LCR, NSFR, ALM gaps, contingency funding plan |
| Interest rate (banking book) | Change in rates affects earnings and the value of equity | Gap and duration analysis |
| Operational | Loss from failed processes, people, systems or external events | Controls, insurance, business continuity |
| Others | Reputation, strategic, compliance, concentration, country risk | Policies and board oversight |

- **Value at Risk (VaR)** – the maximum likely loss over a period at a given confidence level. **Stress tests** check resilience under severe scenarios. **RAROC** = risk-adjusted return ÷ economic capital.

## Credit information and identifiers

- **CRILC** – banks report all borrowers with exposure of **₹5 crore** and above.
- **Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)** – a 20-character global code; required for non-individual borrowers with aggregate exposure of **₹25 crore** and above, and for all RTGS/NEFT payments of **₹50 crore** and above (since 1 April 2021).
- **Credit Information Companies:** TransUnion CIBIL, Equifax, Experian and CRIF High Mark. Lenders report data **fortnightly** (since 1 January 2025); individuals get one **free full credit report** a year from each CIC; delay in resolving a complaint beyond 30 days attracts compensation of **₹100 a day**.

## Quick recap

- LEF: 20% (single) and 25% (group) of Tier 1; large exposure = 10% of Tier 1.
- CME: 40% total, 20% direct; LAS ₹1 crore, IPO ₹25 lakh (1 July 2026).
- PCA triggers: capital, net NPA (6/9/12%), leverage; profitability only monitored; exit after 4 quarters.
- Three lines of defence: business, risk/compliance, internal audit.
- CRILC ₹5 crore; LEI ₹25 crore borrowers and ₹50 crore payments; CIC data fortnightly.
