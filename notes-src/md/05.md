# Customer Service and Grievance Redress

## Grievance redress – the three levels

1. **The bank:** every bank has a Board-approved grievance policy, a nodal officer at each region and a **Principal Nodal Officer** (at least General Manager rank) at head office. Complaints are acknowledged and answered in writing.
2. **Internal Ombudsman (IO):** before a bank rejects a complaint fully or partly, it must refer it to its Internal Ombudsman, an independent senior official. The IO framework (RBI Directions, 2023) applies to commercial banks with 10 or more branches, larger NBFCs, non-bank PPI issuers and credit information companies.
3. **RBI Ombudsman** under the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme.

## Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026

- In force from **1 July 2026**, replacing the RB-IOS 2021 (which itself merged the Banking Ombudsman Scheme 2006, the NBFC Ombudsman Scheme 2018 and the Digital Transactions Ombudsman Scheme 2019 under "One Nation, One Ombudsman"). Complaints received before 1 July 2026 continue under the 2021 scheme.
- **Covered entities:** all commercial banks, RRBs, State and Central Co-operative Banks, scheduled UCBs, non-scheduled UCBs with deposits of **₹50 crore** or more; NBFCs that accept deposits or have a customer interface with assets of **₹100 crore** or more (HFCs, CICs, IFCs, IDF-NBFCs and some others are excluded); non-bank prepaid payment instrument issuers; and credit information companies.
- **Ground:** "deficiency in service" – any shortcoming in a service the entity must provide, whether or not it caused financial loss.

### When can a customer approach the Ombudsman?

- He must first complain **in writing to the bank**, and either get no reply within **30 days**, or be unhappy with the reply, or have the complaint rejected.
- The complaint must be filed directly with the Ombudsman – merely marking a copy to RBI is not enough. It can be filed by the customer or an authorised representative (not an advocate, unless the advocate is the complainant).
- **Not maintainable:** commercial judgment of the bank (e.g., refusing a loan); vendor or outsourcing disputes; grievances against management or executives in general; employer–employee disputes; action taken under orders of a court or statutory authority; matters pending before or decided by a court, tribunal or arbitrator; disputes between regulated entities; complaints made to the bank after the limitation period; services outside RBI's purview.

### Filing and process

- **Online** on the CMS portal (cms.rbi.org.in), by **e-mail** to crpc@rbi.org.in, or **by post** to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC), RBI, Sector 17, **Chandigarh**.
- RBI's contact centre **14448** gives information and status but cannot register complaints.
- The Ombudsman first tries settlement through facilitation or conciliation. The bank must file its reply within the time given; if it does not, the Ombudsman can decide on the available record.
- **No limit** on the amount in dispute. Compensation in an Award: up to **₹30 lakh** for consequential loss, plus up to **₹3 lakh** for loss of time, expenses, harassment and mental anguish (the 2021 limits were ₹20 lakh and ₹1 lakh).
- The complainant must accept the Award in writing within **30 days**, or it lapses. The bank must then comply within 30 days, unless it appeals. Appeals go to the **Appellate Authority** in RBI, generally within 30 days (extendable by another 30).

> **Exam tip:** The Ombudsman cannot award compensation for consequential loss above ₹30 lakh, but there is no ceiling on the disputed amount itself – the bank can be told to refund the full amount.

## Consumer Protection Act, 2019

- Banking is a "service", so customers can approach consumer commissions. The Act also created the **Central Consumer Protection Authority** (2020) and covers e-commerce and mediation.

| Commission | Value of goods/services paid (from 30 December 2021) |
|---|---|
| District | Up to ₹50 lakh |
| State | Above ₹50 lakh and up to ₹2 crore |
| National | Above ₹2 crore |

- Complaint within **2 years** of the cause of action. Appeal from District to State within **45 days**; from State to National and from National to the Supreme Court within **30 days**. Complaints can be filed online (e-Daakhil).

## Unauthorised electronic transactions

These rules (RBI, July 2017) apply until the new directions on small-value digital frauds take effect on 1 January 2027.

| Situation | Customer's liability |
|---|---|
| Fraud due to the bank's negligence or a system breach | Zero, whether or not reported |
| Third-party breach, reported within **3 working days** | Zero |
| Third-party breach, reported in **4–7 working days** | Limited – lower of the transaction value or the cap below |
| Reported after 7 working days | As per the bank's Board-approved policy |
| Customer's own negligence (sharing PIN or OTP) | Full loss until reported; nil after reporting |

| Account | Maximum liability (4–7 days) |
|---|---|
| BSBD account | ₹5,000 |
| Other savings accounts, prepaid instruments, MSME current/CC/OD accounts, individuals' accounts with balance or limit up to ₹25 lakh, credit cards with limit up to ₹5 lakh | ₹10,000 |
| All other current/CC/OD accounts, credit cards with limit above ₹5 lakh | ₹25,000 |

- The bank must give a **shadow credit** within **10 working days** of the report and resolve the complaint within **90 days**.
- **From 1 January 2027:** for small-value digital frauds (loss up to ₹50,000), the customer can get up to 85% of the loss, capped at ₹25,000, once in a lifetime.

## Service rules that are asked

- **Cheque clearing:** cheques are cleared through the **Cheque Truncation System (CTS)**. Since 4 October 2025 (Phase 1), CTS works in **continuous clearing** mode with settlement on realisation, so most cheques are credited the same day. Phase 2, which would cut the bank's confirmation window to 3 hours, has been **postponed until further notice**.
- **Positive Pay:** the drawer shares key details of cheques of **₹50,000 and above** with the bank before presentation; banks may make it compulsory for cheques of ₹5 lakh and above. Only such cheques get the dispute-resolution protection of the CTS grid.
- **Release of property documents:** within **30 days** of full repayment; for delay the lender pays **₹5,000 per day** (since 1 December 2023). If documents are lost, the lender must help get certified copies and bear the cost.
- **Key Facts Statement (KFS):** compulsory for all retail and MSME term loans from 1 October 2024, showing the **Annual Percentage Rate (APR)** and all charges.
- **Penal charges** (from 1 January 2024): lenders may levy only penal *charges*, not penal *interest*, and cannot capitalise them.
- **Floating-rate loans:** borrowers must be offered the option to switch to a fixed rate at reset; from 1 January 2026 banks cannot levy **pre-payment charges** on floating-rate loans to individuals and micro and small enterprises (subject to the limits in RBI's directions).
- **Safe deposit lockers** (revised rules from 1 January 2022): agreement on stamp paper; rent in advance for at most 3 years; the bank is liable up to **100 times the annual rent** for loss due to fire, theft, burglary, building collapse or employee fraud, but not for natural calamities; CCTV footage kept for 180 days; SMS/e-mail alert on every access. A locker not operated for **7 years** whose hirer cannot be traced may be broken open after due process.
- **Minimum balance charges** must be proportionate to the shortfall and cannot make the balance negative.

## Right to Information Act, 2005

- Applies to **public authorities** – RBI and public sector banks (not private banks).
- The Public Information Officer must reply within **30 days** (48 hours where life or liberty is involved). First appeal within 30 days to the first appellate authority; second appeal to the Central Information Commission within **90 days**. Fee ₹10; BPL applicants exempt.
- Sec 8 exempts information held in a fiduciary capacity, commercial confidence and similar matters, but the Supreme Court held (*RBI v. Jayantilal N. Mistry*, 2015) that RBI cannot refuse inspection-related information merely by claiming a fiduciary relationship with banks.

## Banking Codes and Standards Board of India (BCSBI)

- Set up by RBI in 2006 as an independent society to frame voluntary codes – the **Code of Bank's Commitment to Customers** and the **Code for Micro and Small Enterprises** – and to monitor banks' compliance.

## Quick recap

- RB-IOS 2026 from 1 July 2026: ₹30 lakh + ₹3 lakh; CRPC Chandigarh; contact centre 14448.
- First complain to the bank; approach the Ombudsman if no reply in 30 days or unhappy with it.
- Consumer commissions: ₹50 lakh / ₹2 crore; complaint within 2 years.
- Electronic fraud: zero liability within 3 working days; shadow credit in 10 working days; resolve in 90 days.
- Documents after closure: 30 days, else ₹5,000 a day; locker liability 100× rent.

# Taxes and Cash Rules in Banking

## Goods and Services Tax

- Launched on **1 July 2017** under the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**. It subsumed central and state indirect taxes such as excise, service tax and VAT.
- A **dual levy**: CGST and SGST (or UTGST) on supplies within a State; **IGST** on supplies between States and on imports. The **GST Council** (Article 279A) is chaired by the Union Finance Minister.
- **GST 2.0** (effective **22 September 2025**): two main slabs of **5% and 18%**; most items earlier at 12% and 28% were moved into these; a special **40%** rate for luxury and sin goods (tobacco products, pan masala, aerated drinks and similar). Exports remain zero-rated.
- **Banking services** (fees, charges, commissions) attract **18%** GST. Interest on deposits and loans is exempt.

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Registration (normal States) | Turnover above ₹40 lakh for goods; ₹20 lakh for services |
| E-way bill | Movement of goods worth more than ₹50,000 |
| E-invoicing | Businesses with turnover above ₹5 crore (since 1 August 2023) |
| GSTIN | 15 characters: State code (2) + PAN (10) + entity number (1) + "Z" + check digit |

- **Returns:** GSTR-1 (outward supplies) and **GSTR-3B** (summary return with payment). For turnover above ₹5 crore, GSTR-3B is due on the **20th** of the next month; smaller taxpayers under the quarterly scheme file by the 22nd or 24th depending on the State. GSTR-9 is the annual return.

## Income tax – TDS on deposit interest

- From **1 April 2026** the **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act. TDS on interest other than on securities is now under **Section 393(1)** (it was Section 194A). Transactions before that date stay under the old Act.

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Threshold (per bank, per year) | Interest above **₹50,000**; **₹1 lakh** for senior citizens (from 1 April 2025) |
| Rate | 10% for residents; **20%** if PAN is not given (or is inoperative) |
| Deposits covered | Term deposits and recurring deposits (savings interest is not subject to TDS) |
| Joint account | TDS in the name of the first holder |
| Minor's account | Interest is clubbed with the parent's income |
| NRO account | TDS at the rates for non-residents (30% plus surcharge and cess on interest); NRE and FCNR(B) interest is tax-free |
| No-deduction declaration | Self-declaration if income is below the taxable limit (Form 15G; Form 15H for senior citizens under the old Act) |
| Deposit of TDS | By the 7th of the next month (for March, by 30 April); quarterly TDS returns; TDS certificate to the depositor every quarter |

- **TDS on cash withdrawals:** 2% on withdrawals above **₹1 crore** a year from all accounts with a bank; for people who have not filed returns for the last three years, 2% above ₹20 lakh and 5% above ₹1 crore.
- Other common TDS thresholds: payments to contractors – ₹30,000 a payment or ₹1 lakh a year; rent – ₹50,000 a month (from 1 April 2025).

## Cash rules in the Income-tax law

| Rule (1961 Act section) | Limit |
|---|---|
| No loan or deposit to be **accepted** in cash (269SS) | ₹20,000 or more |
| No loan or deposit to be **repaid** in cash (269T) | ₹20,000 or more – penalty equal to the amount |
| No **receipt** of cash by any person (269ST) | ₹2 lakh or more in a day, per transaction or per event (banks exempt) |

These limits continue under the Income-tax Act, 2025 with new section numbers.

## Other points

- **TCS on foreign remittances under LRS:** no TCS up to **₹10 lakh** a year (from 1 April 2025); above that, generally 20%, but lower for education and medical purposes, and nil for education financed by a loan from a financial institution.
- **FATCA / CRS:** banks report accounts held by US persons and other foreign tax residents to the Income-tax Department each year.
- **Bulk deposits:** ₹3 crore and above for commercial banks; ₹1 crore and above for RRBs.
- Records of customer transactions are kept for at least **5 years**.

> **Watch out:** Some notes still say TDS applies above ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for seniors) under Section 194A. The thresholds were raised to ₹50,000 and ₹1 lakh from 1 April 2025, and the section is now 393(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

## Quick recap

- GST 2.0 from 22 September 2025: 5% and 18%, special 40%; banking services 18%.
- Registration ₹40 lakh (goods) / ₹20 lakh (services); e-way bill above ₹50,000; e-invoice above ₹5 crore.
- TDS on deposit interest: above ₹50,000 (₹1 lakh seniors), 10% (20% without PAN), Section 393(1) from 1 April 2026.
- Cash withdrawals above ₹1 crore – 2% TDS.
- Cash loans/deposits of ₹20,000 or more cannot be accepted or repaid in cash.
