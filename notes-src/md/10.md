# Foreign Exchange Basics

## FEMA, 1999

- The **Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999** came into force on **1 June 2000**, replacing FERA, 1973. FEMA is a **civil** law – contraventions attract penalties, not imprisonment (except for non-payment of penalty).

| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| 3 | Dealing in foreign exchange only through authorised persons |
| 5 | **Current account** transactions – free, except those restricted by the Central Government (Current Account Transactions Rules, 2000: Schedule I prohibited, Schedule II need Government approval, Schedule III within limits) |
| 6 | **Capital account** transactions – non-debt instruments regulated by the Government (NDI Rules, 2019); debt instruments by RBI |
| 7 / 8 | Export of goods and services; realisation and repatriation of foreign exchange |
| 10 | Authorised persons |
| 13 | Penalty up to **3 times** the sum involved (₹2 lakh where not quantifiable), plus up to ₹5,000 a day for continuing contravention |
| 15 | Compounding of contraventions (by RBI or the Enforcement Directorate) |

- The **Enforcement Directorate** investigates contraventions; appeals go to the Special Director (Appeals) and then the Appellate Tribunal.

## Authorised persons

| Category | Who | Can do |
|---|---|---|
| AD Category-I | Commercial banks, some co-operative banks | All current and capital account transactions |
| AD Category-II | Upgraded money changers, some co-operative banks and RRBs | Specified non-trade remittances plus money-changing |
| AD Category-III | Select financial institutions | Transactions incidental to their business |
| Full-fledged money changers (FFMCs) | NBFCs and others | Buy foreign currency; sell for private and business travel |

- **Money Transfer Service Scheme (MTSS):** only **inward** personal remittances (e.g., to families) through tie-ups with overseas principals; up to **USD 2,500** per remittance and 30 remittances a year per beneficiary; cash payout up to ₹50,000.

## Exchange rate system

- Par value under Bretton Woods → basket peg (1975) → **LERMS** dual rates (March 1992) → **market-determined unified rate** (1 March 1993) → current account convertibility under IMF **Article VIII** (20 August 1994).
- India follows a **managed float**: the rate is set by the market and RBI intervenes only to curb excess volatility.
- **FEDAI** (Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India, 1958) frames rules for banks' forex business with customers and among themselves. **FBIL** (Financial Benchmarks India Ltd, 2014) publishes the USD/INR reference rate.

## Quotes and rates

- **Direct quote** – home currency per unit of foreign currency (USD 1 = ₹88.50). India has used direct quotes since **2 August 1993**. **Indirect quote** – foreign currency per unit of home currency.
- **Two-way quote:** the bank's buying (bid) rate and selling (ask) rate; the difference is the **spread**. Rule for a direct quote: "**buy low, sell high**".
- **Cross rate:** derived through a common currency, usually USD. Example: EUR/USD 1.10 and USD/INR 88.00 gives EUR/INR = 1.10 × 88.00 = ₹96.80.

| Delivery | Settlement |
|---|---|
| Cash / ready | Same day (T+0) |
| TOM | Next working day (T+1) |
| **Spot** | Second working day (T+2) |
| **Forward** | Any date beyond spot |

- **Forward premium/discount** reflects the interest-rate difference between the two currencies (interest rate parity): the currency with the higher interest rate trades at a forward discount.
- **Forward contracts** may be for a fixed date or an option period. On cancellation, the bank recovers or pays the difference between the contract rate and the reverse rate.
- USD–INR **currency futures** (2008) and **options** (2010) trade on stock exchanges.

### Merchant rates – which rate applies

| Rate | Used for |
|---|---|
| **TT buying** | Inward remittances (TT, MT, drafts) when the nostro is already credited; cancellation of a forward **sale** contract |
| **Bill buying** | Purchase or discount of **export bills** (includes the transit period) |
| **TT selling** | Outward remittances; payment of import bills not handled by the bank; cancellation of a forward **purchase** contract; crystallisation of overdue export bills |
| **Bill selling** | Import bills where the bank handles the documents – the **highest** rate |

> **Exam tip:** An unpaid foreign-currency export bill is **crystallised** into rupees (converted at the TT selling rate) on the **30th day** after its notional due date, so the exporter bears the exchange risk from then on.

## Nostro, vostro and loro

| Account | Meaning (from an Indian bank's view) |
|---|---|
| **Nostro** – "our account with you" | Indian bank's foreign-currency account with a bank abroad |
| **Vostro** – "your account with us" | Foreign bank's rupee account with an Indian bank |
| **Loro** – "their account with you" | Reference to a third bank's account |
| **Mirror account** | The Indian bank's own record of its nostro account |

- **Special Rupee Vostro Accounts (SRVAs)** let partner-country banks settle trade in **INR** (RBI framework, July 2022).
- **NOP-INR:** each AD Category-I bank must keep its net open rupee position in the onshore deliverable market within **USD 100 million** (from 27 March 2026). Swap positions from the 2026 FCNR(B)/ECB swap windows are excluded.

## Categories of forex branches

| Category | Role |
|---|---|
| A | Maintain their own nostro accounts and deal independently |
| B | No nostro accounts, but deal in forex independently through the bank's nostros |
| C | Route all forex business through an A or B category branch |

## Quick recap

- FEMA in force 1 June 2000; civil law; penalty up to 3× the amount.
- AD Cat-I – all transactions; MTSS – inward only, USD 2,500.
- Direct quote since 1993; spot = T+2; buy low, sell high.
- Export bills – bill buying; outward remittance – TT selling; import bills via bank – bill selling.
- Nostro = our account with you; vostro = your account with us; SRVA for INR trade.

# Trade Finance

## Letters of credit (LCs)

- A **documentary credit** is the issuing bank's undertaking to pay the beneficiary (exporter) against documents that comply with the LC terms.
- **Parties:** applicant (importer); issuing bank; beneficiary (exporter); advising bank; confirming bank; nominated or negotiating bank; reimbursing bank.

### UCP 600 (ICC, in force from 1 July 2007; 39 articles)

| Article | Rule |
|---|---|
| 3 | An LC is **irrevocable** even if it does not say so (revocable LCs no longer exist under UCP 600) |
| 14 | Banks get a maximum of **5 banking days** after presentation to examine documents. Documents must be presented within **21 calendar days** after shipment and within the LC's validity |
| 16 | A bank refusing documents must give a single notice of all discrepancies by the end of the fifth banking day |
| 18 | Commercial invoice in the applicant's name, in the LC currency, not over the LC amount |
| 28 | Insurance cover at least **110%** of CIF or CIP value; dated no later than shipment |
| 29 | If the expiry date falls on a bank holiday, it extends to the next banking day |
| 30 | "About" or "approximately" allows **±10%** in amount, quantity or unit price; **±5%** tolerance on quantity when goods are not counted in units |
| 31 | Partial drawings and shipments are allowed unless prohibited |
| 38 | A transferable credit can be transferred **only once** (but to several second beneficiaries if partial shipments are allowed) |

### Other ICC rules

| Rule | Covers |
|---|---|
| eUCP 2.0 | Electronic presentation of documents |
| ISBP 821 | International Standard Banking Practice for examining documents |
| URC 522 | Collections (D/P, D/A) |
| URR 725 | Bank-to-bank reimbursements |
| URDG 758 | Demand guarantees |
| ISP98 | Standby letters of credit |

### Types of LCs

| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Sight / usance | Payment on presentation / on a future date (acceptance or deferred payment) |
| Confirmed | A second bank (usually in the exporter's country) adds its own undertaking |
| **Red clause** | Allows an advance to the exporter **before shipment** (unsecured) |
| **Green clause** | Advance before shipment against goods stored in a warehouse |
| **Revolving** | Amount reinstated automatically after use (cumulative or non-cumulative) |
| **Back-to-back** | A second LC opened by a middleman on the strength of the first (master) LC |
| **Transferable** | The first beneficiary transfers it to a supplier (second beneficiary) |
| **Standby LC** | Works like a guarantee – paid on the applicant's default (ISP98 or UCP) |
| Restricted | Negotiation restricted to a named bank |

- **Collection of bills (URC 522):** **D/P** – documents released against **payment** (sight bills); **D/A** – documents released against **acceptance** of a usance bill. Collections carry more risk than LCs because no bank undertakes to pay.

## Shipping documents

| Bill of lading type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clean / claused (foul) | No adverse remarks / remarks on defective goods or packing – banks accept only clean B/Ls |
| Shipped (on board) / received for shipment | Goods loaded on the vessel / only received by the carrier |
| Order / straight | Negotiable ("to order") / consigned to a named person – not negotiable |
| Through / multimodal | Covers carriage by more than one carrier or mode |
| Charter party B/L | Issued under a charter of the ship (UCP Art 22) |
| House B/L | Issued by a freight forwarder (the carrier issues the master B/L) |

- A bill of lading is a receipt for goods, evidence of the contract of carriage and a **document of title**. An **airway bill**, sea waybill and lorry or railway receipt are **not** documents of title.

## Incoterms 2020

- ICC rules on the delivery point, costs and risk between seller and buyer; **11 terms**, in force from 1 January 2020.

| Group | Terms |
|---|---|
| Any mode of transport (7) | EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, **DPU**, DDP |
| Sea and inland waterway only (4) | FAS, **FOB**, CFR, **CIF** |

- **EXW** – minimum obligation for the seller; **DDP** – maximum (seller delivers duty paid).
- **FOB / CFR / CIF** – risk passes when goods are **on board** the vessel at the port of shipment. Under CIF the seller also pays freight and **minimum insurance (ICC "C")**; under **CIP** the seller must buy **all-risk cover (ICC "A")**.
- **DPU** (delivered at place unloaded) replaced **DAT** in 2020; under FCA the parties may agree that an on-board B/L is issued.

## Export finance

### Pre-shipment (packing credit)

- **Rupee packing credit** against a firm order or LC to buy, process and pack goods; period up to **360 days**; liquidated from export proceeds.
- **PCFC** – pre-shipment credit in foreign currency, at internationally linked rates.
- **Running account facility** – packing credit without a specific order for each drawal, for exporters with a good track record.

### Post-shipment

- Purchase, discount or negotiation of export bills; advances against bills sent on collection; advances against undrawn balances and retention money; export bills rediscounted abroad (EBR).
- Normally up to **365 days** from shipment.

### Support for exporters

| Facility | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Niryat Protsahan** (Export Promotion Mission) | **2.75%** interest subvention on rupee pre- and post-shipment credit to MSME exporters, from 2 January 2026 (the earlier Interest Equalisation Scheme ended on 31 December 2024) |
| **Gold Card Scheme** (2004) | Better terms (in-principle limits for 3 years, faster processing, stand-by limits) for creditworthy exporters with a clean track record |
| **ECGC** (1957) | Export credit insurance – policies for exporters and **ECIB** cover for banks' packing and post-shipment credit |
| **EXIM Bank** (1982) | Term finance for exports, **lines of credit** to foreign governments and buyer's credit |
| **Diamond Dollar Account** | For diamond traders with at least 2 years' track record and average turnover of ₹3 crore or more; up to 5 accounts |
| **EEFC account** | Exporters can keep **100%** of forex earnings in foreign currency; non-interest bearing |

## Realisation and monitoring

- **FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026** – in force **1 October 2026**. Export proceeds must be realised and repatriated within **9 months** of export (**12 months** if invoiced or settled in INR), after the amendment of 22 September 2026.
- **EDPMS** (Export Data Processing and Monitoring System, 2014) tracks every shipping bill until realised; **IDPMS** (2016) tracks import payments against bills of entry. Entries up to **₹10 lakh** per bill can be closed on the exporter's or importer's declaration.
- **Merchanting trade** (goods bought in one country and sold to another without entering India): forex outlay allowed for up to **6 months**; the transaction must be completed within **9 months**.

## Foreign Trade Policy 2023

- In force from **1 April 2023**, with no fixed end date (DGFT). Focus on remission schemes (RoDTEP), INR trade settlement, e-commerce exports and Towns of Export Excellence.

| Status | Export performance (USD) |
|---|---|
| One Star Export House | 3 million |
| Two Star | 15 million |
| Three Star | 50 million |
| Four Star | 200 million |
| Five Star | 800 million |

- **EPCG:** zero-duty import of capital goods, with an export obligation of **6 times** the duty saved within 6 years. **Advance Authorisation:** duty-free import of inputs for export production.

## Quick recap

- UCP 600: irrevocable; 5 banking days; 21 days after shipment; insurance 110%; transfer only once.
- Red clause – advance before shipment; back-to-back – middleman; standby – guarantee.
- Incoterms 2020: 11 terms; DPU replaced DAT; CIP needs all-risk cover.
- Packing credit up to 360 days; post-shipment up to 365 days; crystallise on 30th day.
- Export realisation 9 months (12 if INR) from 1 October 2026; EDPMS/IDPMS ₹10 lakh.

# NRI Accounts, Residents' Forex and LRS

## Who is a non-resident

- Under FEMA, a person resident outside India includes one who has gone abroad for employment, business or any purpose showing intention to stay outside India for an uncertain period. A person staying in India **182 days or more** in the preceding financial year is generally resident.
- **NRI** – Indian citizen resident outside India; **OCI / PIO** – person of Indian origin holding a foreign passport.

## Accounts for non-residents

| Point | NRE | NRO | FCNR(B) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Currency | Rupees | Rupees | Freely convertible foreign currency |
| Types | Savings, current, RD, term deposits | Savings, current, RD, term deposits | **Term deposits only, 1–5 years** |
| Source of funds | Remittances from abroad, transfers from other NRE/FCNR accounts | Remittances and **local income** (rent, dividend, pension) | Remittances from abroad |
| Repatriation | **Fully** repatriable | Up to **USD 1 million** per financial year (after tax) | Fully repatriable |
| Interest | **Tax-free** | **Taxable** (TDS) | Tax-free |
| Exchange risk | Depositor | Depositor | **Bank** |
| Joint holding | With NRIs, or with a resident relative on "former or survivor" basis | With residents too | With NRIs, or resident relative ("former or survivor") |

- **Loans** can be given in India or abroad against NRE/FCNR(B) deposits to the depositor or third parties.
- **SNRR account** (Special Non-Resident Rupee) – for non-residents with bona fide business interests in India; rupee account, repatriable, non-interest bearing.
- **Foreign tourists** may open an NRO account during their stay (up to 6 months).
- An NRI can repatriate sale proceeds of up to **two** residential properties, subject to conditions.

> **2026 update:** Fresh FCNR(B) deposits of 3–5 years (from 8 June 2026) and fresh NRE term deposits of 3 years and above (from 19 June 2026) were exempt from CRR and SLR until 31 August 2026, and banks could swap FCNR(B) dollars with RBI.

## Foreign currency accounts of residents

| Account | Who / what |
|---|---|
| **EEFC** | Exporters and other foreign exchange earners – up to 100% of earnings; current account, no interest |
| **RFC** | Returning NRIs – foreign currency assets brought back; freely usable and repatriable |
| **RFC(D)** | Any resident – foreign exchange received as gifts, honorarium, unspent travel money; current account, no interest, no limit |

- A resident may keep up to **USD 2,000** in foreign currency notes and travellers' cheques; unspent foreign exchange from a trip is to be surrendered within **180 days** of return.
- Foreign currency for travel: up to **USD 3,000** per trip in currency notes; the rest on cards or travellers' cheques.
- Up to **₹50,000** of foreign currency can be bought against cash.

## Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)

- Resident individuals, **including minors**, can remit up to **USD 2,50,000 per financial year** for permitted current and capital account purposes – travel, education, medical treatment, gifts, maintenance of relatives, investment in shares, debt and property abroad, opening foreign currency accounts abroad.
- **Not allowed:** margin trading or forex trading abroad, lottery and gambling, items banned for import, remittances to countries identified by FATF as non-co-operative.
- PAN is required; **TCS** applies on remittances above ₹10 lakh a year (see Chapter 10).

## Cross-border remittance facilities

- **Indo-Nepal Remittance Facility** (2008, through SBI and NEFT): up to **₹50,000** per remittance and **12 remittances a year** in cash for persons without an account; account-to-account transfers up to ₹2 lakh per transaction.
- **Rupee lending:** AD banks and their overseas branches may lend in INR to residents of **Bhutan, Nepal and Sri Lanka** for cross-border trade (2025-26).

## Depository receipts

| Receipt | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ADR** | American Depository Receipt – Indian company's shares listed in the US |
| **GDR** | Global Depository Receipt – listed in Europe (London, Luxembourg) |
| **IDR** | Indian Depository Receipt – a foreign company's shares listed in India (Standard Chartered PLC was the first, 2010) |

## Quick recap

- NRE – rupee, repatriable, tax-free; NRO – local income, USD 1 million a year, taxable; FCNR(B) – foreign currency, 1–5 years, bank bears exchange risk.
- EEFC 100% for exporters; RFC for returning NRIs; RFC(D) for residents' small forex receipts.
- LRS USD 2.5 lakh per year; minors included; TCS above ₹10 lakh.
- Indo-Nepal: ₹50,000 per remittance, 12 a year.
- Resident can hold USD 2,000; surrender within 180 days; travel cash USD 3,000.
